# MAN-1: Extra dimensions offer no hyperspace

*"Hyperspace" as a separate space with laxer rules has no counterpart in physics.*

**Kind:** constraint · **Family:** MAN (Leaving the manifold) · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The result

Extra dimensions, where they appear in physics, are compact (Kaluza–Klein; large and compact in ADD, with Standard Model fields localised on a wall) or warped (Randall–Sundrum, a slice of anti-de Sitter space with the Standard Model on a brane). Shortcuts through the bulk are possible: Chung and Freese show a signal can leave the brane along a bulk null geodesic and return elsewhere sooner than any signal confined to the brane, as a non-inflationary answer to the horizon problem; Ishihara shows matter on the brane bends it so that such shortcuts exist, and calls the result apparent causality violation on the brane, with true causality defined by the bulk light cone

## What it forbids or costs

"Hyperspace" as a separate space with laxer rules has no counterpart in physics. Bulk shortcuts are a form of L3, remain inside the bulk light cone, and look to a brane observer like FTL with all of CAU-1's consequences unless the bulk supplies a preferred frame (CAU-4, an inference of this register, not a claim of these papers)

## Escape hatches

Brane bending is a real research direction; the price is that brane physics is no longer the whole story

## Who it bites

**Physics proposals**

- VIOLATES: [Heim theory and the Dröscher-Häuser propulsion proposal (2004 to 2005)](../theories/physics/heim-theory.md). A parallel space $\mathbb R^4(n)$ with its own light speed $nc$, its own $G/n$ and its own clock $t/n^3$, entered by a vehicle and exited displaced, is exactly the "hyperspace as a separate space with laxer rules" that the entry says has no counterpart in physics; the proposal requires it.
- DODGES: [NASA Eagleworks warp field interferometer](../theories/physics/eagleworks-warp-field-interferometer.md). Leans on the Chung–Freese bulk as an informal analogue of the boost, $\gamma \sim e^U$, and on oscillating $dU/dt$ to cut the energy bill; the entry's finding is that bulk shortcuts are a form of L3 inside the bulk light cone, not a dial on the warp bill, and no calculation connects the two
- SATISFIES: [Brane-bulk shortcuts (Chung and Freese 2000, Ishihara 2001, Caldwell and Langlois 2001)](../theories/physics/brane-bulk-shortcuts.md). This is the entry's second half stated exactly: the shortcut is real, is a form of L3, remains inside the bulk light cone and looks from the brane like FTL, and the size is set by $(\ell H)^2$.
- SATISFIES: [Kaluza-Klein and the hyperspace reading (Kaluza 1921, Klein 1926, ADD 1998, Randall-Sundrum 1999)](../theories/physics/kaluza-klein-hyperspace.md). This is the entry's first half stated exactly: extra dimensions where they appear are compact or warped, Standard Model fields are localised, and "hyperspace" as a separate space with laxer rules has no counterpart; the frameworks are what the entry describes and the hyperspace reading is what it excludes.
- SATISFIES: [Maldacena, Milekhin and Popov: traversable wormholes in four dimensions (2018), and the humanly traversable sequel (2021)](../theories/physics/maldacena-milekhin-popov-2018.md). The human version uses a Randall–Sundrum II bulk exactly as MAN-1 describes such models, and the bulk supplies degrees of freedom, not a shortcut: the wormhole remains longer than the brane path, so no apparent brane causality violation arises.

**Fictional drives**

- SILENT: [2001: A Space Odyssey (1968): the Star Gate](../theories/fiction/2001-star-gate.md). The makers "store knowledge in the structure of space itself" and the hotel room is somewhere unplaced; the work hints at other spaces and never names an extra dimension or a bulk, so the entry's physics is not invoked.
- SILENT: [Contact: the Machine and the tunnel system (Sagan 1985, Zemeckis 1997)](../theories/fiction/contact-sagan-machine.md). The tunnels are not described as leaving the four-dimensional manifold; the passengers argue about singularities, not extra dimensions, and the film says only "wormhole".
- SILENT: [Cowboy Bebop (1998): the astral gates](../theories/fiction/cowboy-bebop-astral-gates.md). The tunnel is called hyperspace and "Gateway Shuffle" gives it one property of a separate space, that a ship can be lost in it; the series never says what it is, gives it no dimensions and no physics, so the entry's content is not engaged.
- SILENT: [Dune: the Guild navigators and folding space (Herbert 1965)](../theories/fiction/dune-holtzman-fold-space.md). "Folding space" could be read as passage through a higher dimension and the 1984 narration does not say; the 1965 text says nothing at all about dimensions.
- SILENT: [EVE Online: the warp drive (CCP Games, 2003)](../theories/fiction/eve-online-warp-drive.md). The stargate article invokes hyperspace and dimensional interconnections without a mechanism; the register's finding that no such space exists is not engaged.
- SILENT: [Event Horizon (1997): the gravity drive](../theories/fiction/event-horizon-gravity-drive.md). Weir calls the gateway "dimensional" and the destination is outside the known universe, which is a bulk-shortcut claim; the film gives it no structure, no extra dimension named, no return rule, and uses the other place only as the source of the horror.
- SILENT: [Farscape (1999): wormholes, and starburst as a separate thing](../theories/fiction/farscape-wormholes.md). Einstein's people live in a "realm" that wormholes alone traverse, and the Ancients are modified to live in ours; the show posits other spaces and gives them no dimensions, no bulk and no physics, so the entry is not engaged.
- SILENT: [Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2 (2017): jump points](../theories/fiction/guardians-jump-points.md). The bodily distortion in transit hints at passage through somewhere with different rules and the film gives that somewhere no name, no dimension and no physics.
- SILENT: [Hyperion: the farcasters (Simmons 1989)](../theories/fiction/hyperion-farcasters.md). Where the Core lives, "inside" the farcaster network, is a place the novels never locate in or out of ordinary space; the sequels give it a name and the first two books do not, so the question of an extra dimension is raised by implication and never answered.
- SILENT: [Star Citizen: quantum drive and jump points](../theories/fiction/star-citizen-jump-points.md). Interspace is described as higher-dimensional in wiki summaries of developer talks and as "a smear of sight and sound" in the lore; no dimension is counted and no bulk physics is invoked.
- SILENT: [Star Trek: warp drive (1966 onward)](../theories/fiction/star-trek-warp-drive.md). Subspace is a domain beneath normal space with its own rules and is never reconciled with compact or warped extra dimensions; the show uses it as a word.
- SILENT: [Starfield (2023): the grav drive](../theories/fiction/starfield-grav-drive.md). The Unity and the Starborn imply other universes reached through the artefacts, and the drive itself was derived from an artefact; the game never connects the jump to another dimension or bulk, and gives the other universes no physics.
- SILENT: [Super Dimension Fortress Macross (1982): the space fold](../theories/fiction/macross-fold-drive.md). "Super dimension" is the series' name for what a fold passes through, and in 1982 it is only a name: no extra dimension is described, no bulk, no rule for entry or exit.
- SILENT: [The Expanse: the ring gates and the slow zone (Corey 2013 onward)](../theories/fiction/expanse-ring-gates.md). The hub is described as a space outside any system, starless and bounded, but the novels never say whether it is elsewhere in our manifold or outside it; the question is not raised.
- SILENT: [The Orville: quantum drive (2017 onward)](../theories/fiction/the-orville-quantum-drive.md). The 2019 production description of quantum space as a higher-dimensional realm would put the drive here; no episode says so and the register's finding, that no such realm exists, is not engaged.
- SILENT: [Thor (2011): the Bifrost](../theories/fiction/thor-bifrost.md). The Nine Realms are presented as branches of Yggdrasil in Thor's drawing for Jane, which is a picture of other spaces rather than of far places in one space; the film never says whether the realms are elsewhere in our universe or elsewhere altogether, and the physics of a bulk is not invoked.
- ENGAGES: [Andromeda: the slipstream](../theories/fiction/andromeda-slipstream.md). The setting states the other space and its rules on screen: strings through an additional dimension, unnavigable by machines, with travel time unrelated to distance. MAN-1 says physics supplies no such space; the show's answer is the organic-observer rule, which is the laxer rule named.
- ENGAGES: [Asimov's Foundation: the Jump through hyperspace (1942 onward)](../theories/fiction/foundation-hyperspace-jumps.md). "Neither space nor time, matter nor energy, something nor nothing" is the entry's "separate space with laxer rules" defined by negation; the series depicts exactly the thing the entry says physics does not supply, and says so in its first chapter.
- ENGAGES: [Babylon 5: hyperspace, jump gates and beacons (1993 to 1998)](../theories/fiction/babylon-5-hyperspace.md). The show depicts a separate space with shorter distances, entered from our space by an opened point and left at a chosen point, which is the entry's "hyperspace as a separate space with laxer rules" drawn in full and given weather.
- ENGAGES: [Banks's Culture: the energy grid, infraspace and ultraspace (1987 onward)](../theories/fiction/culture-hyperspace-grid.md). Two separate spaces with their own rules, a bulk between universes with an energy grid in it and ships that push on it: the entry's "separate space with laxer rules" built as a cosmology, with the author's own note that it is invented.
- ENGAGES: [Campbell's Islands of Space (1931): where the word came from](../theories/fiction/word-hyperspace-campbell-1931.md). The novel coins the travel sense of the word and then uses it for a self-made pocket rather than a separate space; it is the one work in this class whose "hyperspace" is not the thing MAN-1 says has no counterpart.
- ENGAGES: [Cordwainer Smith: planoforming, Space² and Space³](../theories/fiction/cordwainer-smith-planoforming.md). The work names the move: planoform ships "drop one of our familiar dimensions and pick up a new dimension" ("Drunkboat"), and Space³ is a space with no distance at all. MAN-1 says a separate space with laxer rules has no counterpart in physics; Smith's stories are among the few that say what the laxer rules are (no distance) and what they cost (the mind).
- ENGAGES: [Elite Dangerous: the frame shift drive (Frontier Developments, 2014)](../theories/fiction/elite-dangerous-frame-shift-drive.md). Witch-space is another space with its own rules, entered and left on screen on every jump; the 1984 original's mis-jumps into it are part of the series' identity. Engaged as a premise, never as physics.
- ENGAGES: [Halo: slipspace and the Shaw-Fujikawa translight engine (2001 onward)](../theories/fiction/halo-slipspace.md). The franchise borrows the number of dimensions from physics (eleven) and then gives them exactly the properties MAN-1 says extra dimensions do not have: a space to be entered, with its own physics, its own inconsistent time and shorter effective distances. It is the entry's counterexample-by-fiction with the physics' own label on it.
- ENGAGES: [Interstellar: the wormhole near Saturn (2014)](../theories/fiction/interstellar-wormhole.md). The film's "they" live in the bulk and the tesseract is a five-dimensional construction; Professor Brand's equation concerns gravity across dimensions, and the companion book ties this to the Randall–Sundrum picture in which gravity alone propagates in the bulk. The film uses the extra dimension as the seat of the builders rather than as a route for the ship, so the wormhole stays L3 while its explanation is MAN-1's territory.
- ENGAGES: [Niven's Known Space: the Outsider hyperdrive and the blind spot (1966 onward)](../theories/fiction/known-space-hyperdrive.md). A separate space with its own speed, its own effect on the eye and its own inhabitants, entered at will away from masses: the entry's "separate space with laxer rules" as depicted, and the mass rule is the one feature that faintly echoes what brane physics says happens near matter.
- ENGAGES: [Star Trek: transwarp, quantum slipstream and the other beyond-warp drives](../theories/fiction/star-trek-transwarp-and-slipstream.md). Transwarp space, the quantum barrier and the mycelial plane are all other spaces with their own rules, named and used on screen; "Context Is for Kings" defines the mycelial network as a structure spanning the cosmos. Engaged as a premise, never as physics.
- ENGAGES: [Star Wars: hyperspace (1977 onward)](../theories/fiction/star-wars-hyperspace.md). The films depict, wholesale, the thing the entry says has no counterpart: a separate space with its own distances, entered at will from any point in ours and left at a chosen point. The Holdo manoeuvre even shows the ship in both spaces at once. This is the class the entry was written to name.
- ENGAGES: [Stellaris: warp, hyperlane and wormhole (Paradox Development Studio, 2016)](../theories/fiction/stellaris-three-modes.md). Hyperlanes exist in subspace and are the game's default and now only baseline mode; the Unbidden arrive from another dimension when the fabric is strained. Another space with its own rules is a premise stated in the diary and enacted in play, never as physics.
- ENGAGES: [Vinge's Zones of Thought: FTL only where physics permits it (1992)](../theories/fiction/vinge-zones-of-thought.md). The novel's answer to "hyperspace has no counterpart in physics" is to agree for our neighbourhood and to move the counterpart elsewhere: FTL is admitted only where the laws are stated to differ, and the region where they do not differ is called the Slowness and contains Earth. The entry's finding is built into the map.
- ENGAGES: [Warhammer 40,000: the Warp (1987 onward)](../theories/fiction/warhammer-40k-the-warp.md). The Warp is the entry's separate space with laxer rules taken to its limit: different physics, different inhabitants and a different time. The setting depicts what MAN-1 says has no counterpart and, unusually, charges itself for the clock.
- ENGAGES: [Weber's Honorverse: hyper bands and Warshawski sails (1993 onward)](../theories/fiction/honorverse-hyperspace.md). A stack of separate spaces, each with a stated multiplier on distance, entered by translation and left at a chosen point: the entry's "separate space with laxer rules" made numerical, and the one hyperspace in the catalogue with a fringe physics proposal (Heim's $nc$ parallel spaces) that resembles it.
- ENGAGES: [Xenocide: travel through the Outside](../theories/fiction/xenocide-outside.md). The novel states the other space and its rules: Outside has no space and no time, every philote is equally distant from every other, and re-entry is at will. MAN-1 says physics supplies no such space; the novel's answer is to make its space prior to physics, and to say so.
- RULE: [Doctor Who: the TARDIS and the time vortex](../theories/fiction/doctor-who-tardis.md). The stated rule is that the vortex is outside spacetime and its rules differ ("The Time Monster", 1972 onward), which is exactly the separate space with laxer rules MAN-1 says physics does not supply. The rule answers the row by assertion.
- RULE: [FTL: Faster Than Light, the jump drive as a game mechanic](../theories/fiction/ftl-the-game.md). The stated rule is that an FTL jump moves the ship from beacon to beacon with no transit through space, which is the manifold-leaving move in its barest form. MAN-1 says physics supplies no such move; the game's answer is a charge bar.
- RULE: [Homeworld: hyperspace cores and the Far Jumpers](../theories/fiction/homeworld-hyperspace-cores.md). The stated rule is that a core opens a window into hyperspace through which a ship leaves the field of play and re-enters elsewhere, with the Far Jumpers covering the galaxy in almost no time. MAN-1 says physics supplies no such window; the games' answer is a blue rectangle and a treaty forbidding its use.
- RULE: [No Man's Sky: the hyperdrive, warp cells and range](../theories/fiction/no-mans-sky-hyperdrive.md). The stated rule is that the hyperdrive takes the ship through a tunnel outside the galaxy's space to a system within a range measured in light-years. MAN-1 says physics supplies no such tunnel; the game's answer is a range figure and a fuel gauge.
- RULE: [Space Battleship Yamato: warp by wave motion engine](../theories/fiction/yamato-wave-motion-warp.md). The stated rule is the passage "between the third dimension and the fourth" during a warp (episode 4), with a calculated re-entry point. MAN-1 says no extra dimension physics knows is a place to leap through; the series' answer is a light show and a chart.
- RULE: [Star Trek subspace radio](../theories/fiction/star-trek-subspace-radio.md). Subspace is the stated separate domain that carries the signal; the register says such a space has no physical counterpart and that bulk shortcuts are FTL with CAU-1's consequences unless the bulk supplies a frame
- RULE: [Stargate: the ships' hyperdrives](../theories/fiction/stargate-hyperdrive.md). The stated rule is that hyperspace is a region with its own rules, entered through a window, invisible from normal space and traversed at builder-dependent speeds. MAN-1 says physics supplies no such region; the franchise's answer is a speed ladder, which is a rule and not a mechanism.
- RULE: [The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy: the Infinite Improbability Drive and bistromathics](../theories/fiction/hitchhikers-improbability-drive.md). The work's rule is that the drive avoids hyperspace altogether and is "everywhere" instead, which is the Guide's entry (chapter 10). MAN-1 says no separate space with laxer rules exists in physics; the drive's answer is that it does not use a space at all. The class is answered by declining it, which is the joke.
