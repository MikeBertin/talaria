# The constraint register

*Every published result that puts a price on beating light, with what it forbids and who it bites.*

**Status: v1 DRAFT (M0, 2026-09-12).** Every entry was written from working knowledge in one session. Citations are marked ⚠ v1 until M1 verifies each one against the paper. Treat the statements as probably right and not yet checked.

Verdict vocabulary for the dossiers: physics proposals **SATISFY**, **VIOLATE**, **DODGE** or are **SILENT** on each entry, or the entry is **N/A**. Fiction **ENGAGES**, has a **RULE**, or is **SILENT**.

## CAU: Causality

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| CAU-1 | In special relativity any signal faster than light in one inertial frame travels backwards in time in some other inertial frame. Two such signals, exchanged between observers in relative motion, deliver a reply before the question was sent: the tachyonic antitelephone | Any FTL signal or vehicle, of any mechanism, unless Lorentz invariance is broken. This is the constraint every loophole must answer | A preferred frame (CAU-4); FTL that only works in one direction or between fixed endpoints; accepting closed timelike curves | Einstein, Ann. Phys. 23, 371 (1907), §5; Tolman, *The Theory of the Relativity of Motion* (1917), pp. 54–55; Benford, Book and Newcomb, Phys. Rev. D 2, 263 (1970) ⚠ v1 |
| CAU-2 | Chronology protection conjecture: the laws of physics prevent the appearance of closed timelike curves, because vacuum polarisation diverges at the chronology horizon | Any scheme that turns FTL into a time machine, which by CAU-1 and CAU-3 is all of them given two devices | A conjecture, not a theorem; semiclassical, so a full quantum gravity might not obey it | Hawking, Phys. Rev. D 46, 603 (1992) ⚠ v1 |
| CAU-3 | Two wormhole mouths, or two warp bubbles, in relative motion form a time machine. One traversable wormhole with one mouth taken on a relativistic round trip does it alone | Wormholes and warp drives are not merely fast: they are time machines with an assembly step | Chronology protection (CAU-2) if it holds | Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever, Phys. Rev. Lett. 61, 1446 (1988); Everett, Phys. Rev. D 53, 7365 (1996) ⚠ v1 |
| CAU-4 | FTL signalling is compatible with causality only if there is a preferred frame in which all FTL signals are future-directed: Lorentz invariance is broken, at least for the FTL sector | Any FTL scheme that keeps full Lorentz invariance is a time machine. Any that avoids the time machine has picked a frame | This is the escape hatch. Its cost is that special relativity is no longer the whole story | Liberati, Sonego and Visser, Ann. Phys. 298, 167 (2002), arXiv:gr-qc/0107091 ⚠ v1 |
| CAU-5 | No-communication theorem: measurements on one half of an entangled pair cannot transmit information to the other half. The marginal statistics at either end are independent of what is done at the other | Every "entanglement radio" and quantum ansible | None within quantum mechanics. Nonlinear modifications of quantum mechanics would allow signalling and are constrained for that reason | Eberhard, Nuovo Cimento B 46, 392 (1978); Ghirardi, Rimini and Weber, Lett. Nuovo Cimento 27, 293 (1980); Peres and Terno, Rev. Mod. Phys. 76, 93 (2004) ⚠ v1 |

## ENE: Energy

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| ENE-1 | The energy conditions. Null (NEC): energy density seen along any light ray is non-negative. Weak (WEC): energy density seen by any observer is non-negative. Averaged null (ANEC): the integral along a complete null geodesic is non-negative. All classical matter obeys them; quantum fields violate the pointwise ones and the ANEC only under restrictions | Not itself a no-go, but the currency every other ENE entry is priced in | Quantum fields (ENE-3, ENE-6) | Hawking and Ellis, *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time* (1973), §4.3; Visser, *Lorentzian Wormholes* (1995), ch. 12; Curiel, in *Towards a Theory of Spacetime Theories* (2017), arXiv:1405.0403 ⚠ v1 |
| ENE-2 | Superluminal travel requires negative energy. Olum: any spacetime allowing a signal to arrive earlier than light through flat space must violate the WEC somewhere along the way. Visser, Bassett and Liberati: the same for the ANEC on the fastest null geodesic, "superluminal censorship". Gao and Wald: no gravitational time advance under the ANEC plus generic conditions | Every L2 and L3 scheme pays in negative energy; positive-energy warp drives cannot be superluminal | Definitions of "superluminal" that compare against a curved rather than flat background; modified theories of gravity | Olum, Phys. Rev. Lett. 81, 3567 (1998), arXiv:gr-qc/9805003; Visser, Bassett and Liberati, Nucl. Phys. B Proc. Suppl. 88, 267 (2000), arXiv:gr-qc/9810026; Gao and Wald, Class. Quantum Grav. 17, 4999 (2000), arXiv:gr-qc/0007021 ⚠ v1 |
| ENE-3 | Quantum inequalities: a quantum field can have negative energy density, but the magnitude times the duration squared is bounded, roughly by the Planck constant. The more negative, the shorter it lasts. Applied to the Alcubierre bubble, the wall must be thinner than about a hundred Planck lengths and the total negative energy is of order 10^62 kg for a bubble large enough to hold a ship, some ten orders of magnitude beyond the mass of the visible universe | Any L2 scheme at engineering scale; the bill is not merely large but larger than the universe | Van Den Broeck's pocket geometry (ENE-4); the inequalities are derived for free fields and specific sampling functions, and interacting fields are less constrained | Ford and Roman, Phys. Rev. D 51, 4277 (1995); Ford and Roman, Phys. Rev. D 55, 2082 (1997); Pfenning and Ford, Class. Quantum Grav. 14, 1743 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9702026 ⚠ v1 (numbers to be re-derived in M1) |
| ENE-4 | A bubble whose interior is a large pocket behind a microscopic throat reduces the Alcubierre negative energy from universe scale to of order a solar mass, at the price of a throat near the Planck scale and an additional positive energy of comparable size | Does not remove the negative energy, only its quantity. The Planck-scale throat is its own bill | None needed; this is itself an escape from ENE-3 | Van Den Broeck, Class. Quantum Grav. 16, 3973 (1999), arXiv:gr-qc/9905084 ⚠ v1 (number to be checked) |
| ENE-5 | Generic warp drives violate the null energy condition. For the general class of warp metrics with a flat interior and flat exterior, the NEC is violated somewhere in the wall for any nonzero velocity, superluminal or not. The 2021 positive-energy solutions either are not superluminal, are not warp drives in the sense of carrying a flat pocket, or contain an error | The positive-energy warp claims of 2021 are not what the press said they were | Metrics outside the generic class; the authors' own subluminal positive-energy constant-velocity solution of 2024 (see LOR-5) | Santiago, Schuster and Visser, Phys. Rev. D 105, 064038 (2022), arXiv:2105.03079 ⚠ v1 |
| ENE-6 | The Casimir effect is real negative energy density between conducting plates, measured to a few percent. Its magnitude is tiny: of order 10^-3 J per cubic metre at micron separations, falling as the fourth power of the gap | The only negative energy anyone has ever made, and the only one anyone can point to, and it is twenty-something orders of magnitude short of any bill on this page | None; this is a floor, not a ceiling | Casimir, Proc. K. Ned. Akad. Wet. 51, 793 (1948); Lamoreaux, Phys. Rev. Lett. 78, 5 (1997) ⚠ v1 |

## CON: Control

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| CON-1 | A superluminal warp bubble has horizons. The crew inside cannot send a signal to the front wall, so they cannot steer, accelerate or stop the bubble from inside once it exceeds c | The pilot's chair is decorative. Control must come from outside, ahead of the bubble | Subluminal warp drives have no horizon; an external agent along the route (CON-2) | Hiscock, Class. Quantum Grav. 14, L183 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9707024; Krasnikov, Phys. Rev. D 57, 4760 (1998), arXiv:gr-qc/9511068 ⚠ v1 |
| CON-2 | The bootstrap problem. The exotic matter that shapes the bubble must already be in place along the whole route before the trip; a ship cannot create the metric ahead of itself faster than light because that would itself be a superluminal signal. Coule: the energy to build the route must itself travel at or below c, so the first traversal is never faster than light | "Just build a warp drive" is not a plan. Warp travel is infrastructure, like a railway, laid at sublight speed | The Krasnikov tube (CON-3), which accepts this and makes the return trip fast | Krasnikov, Phys. Rev. D 57, 4760 (1998); Coule, Class. Quantum Grav. 15, 2523 (1998) ⚠ v1 |
| CON-3 | The Krasnikov tube: a metric laid down along the outbound sublight journey that makes the return trip arbitrarily short. Everett and Roman show it needs negative energy in quantities comparable to the Alcubierre bubble, with wall thickness constrained by the quantum inequalities, and that two tubes make a time machine | The honest L2 scheme, and it still pays the ENE-3 bill and the CAU-3 bill | None beyond those of ENE-3 | Krasnikov, Phys. Rev. D 57, 4760 (1998); Everett and Roman, Phys. Rev. D 56, 2100 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9702049 ⚠ v1 |

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| STA-1 | Semiclassical instability of warp bubbles. Hiscock: the renormalised stress-energy of a quantum field diverges at the bubble horizon for superluminal speed. Finazzi, Liberati and Barceló: a dynamically formed bubble emits Hawking-like radiation, and at superluminal speed the flux inside grows without bound, so the interior is cooked and the wall destabilised | A superluminal bubble is not a stable object even if you could build it | Subluminal bubbles are stable in this analysis; full quantum gravity unknown | Hiscock, Class. | STA-2 | A traversable wormhole throat must be held open by matter that violates the NEC: the flaring-out condition at the throat requires negative energy density along the null rays that thread it. | STA-3 | Divergence at the chronology horizon: when a wormhole is converted into a time machine, the vacuum stress-energy of quantum fields diverges on the Cauchy horizon, which is the mechanism behind Hawking's conjecture. ## LOR: Kinematics

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| LOR-1 | A massive body cannot be accelerated to c: its kinetic energy grows without bound as v approaches c. Nothing that starts below c crosses it by acceleration | Every L1 scheme that proposes to push harder | Never having been below c (tachyons, LOR-2); changing the geometry instead of the velocity (L2, L3) | Einstein, Ann. Phys. 17, 891 (1905); any relativity text ⚠ v1 |
| LOR-2 | Tachyons. A particle with imaginary rest mass would always travel faster than light and could never slow below it. In quantum field theory a field with negative mass squared does not describe faster-than-light particles; it signals an unstable vacuum that rolls to a stable one, after which the excitations are ordinary. Tachyonic particles would in any case give the antitelephone (CAU-1) | L1 by particle physics is closed: the tachyon is an instability, not a courier | None found in forty years of searching; Lorentz-violating frameworks | Bilaniuk, Deshpande and Sudarshan, Am. J. Phys. 30, 718 (1962); Feinberg, Phys. Rev. 159, 1089 (1967); Aharonov, Komar and Susskind, Phys. Rev. 182, 1400 (1969) ⚠ v1 |
| LOR-3 | Phase velocity and group velocity may exceed c; the front velocity of a signal cannot. Sommerfeld and Brillouin showed the wavefront in any causal medium travels at exactly c. Tunnelling "superluminality" (the Hartman effect, the Nimtz experiments, the gain-assisted pulses of Wang et al.) is a reshaping of the pulse envelope, not a signal beating its own front | Every L5 claim built on a group velocity or a tunnelling time | None; this is settled | Brillouin, *Wave Propagation and Group Velocity* (1960); Steinberg, Kwiat and Chiao, Phys. ## MAN: Leaving the manifold

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| MAN-1 | Extra dimensions, where they appear in physics, are either compact at tiny scales (Kaluza–Klein) or warped so that ordinary matter is confined to a brane (Randall–Sundrum, ADD). ## Reading the register

Three entries do almost all the work. CAU-1 says any FTL is a message to the past unless something breaks Lorentz invariance. ENE-2 says any geometric FTL costs negative energy. CON-2 says the negative energy has to be laid along the route in advance at sublight speed. A proposal that answers those three has answered the field; the rest of the register is what it costs once it has.

The register is deliberately physics-internal. It does not score plausibility, engineering readiness or taste. Those live in the dossiers.
