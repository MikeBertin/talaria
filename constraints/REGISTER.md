# The constraint register

*Every published result that puts a price on beating light, with what it forbids and who it bites.*

**Status: v2 VERIFIED (M1, 2026-09-12).** Every citation was checked against the arXiv record, the journal DOI record or INSPIRE, and every stated result against the paper's abstract or text, by four independent verification passes. ✓ marks a verified entry; a residual ⚠ v1 inside a row names the one item in it that could not be opened (paywalled books, two 2026 preprints found by search). M1 corrections: Einstein 1907 was the wrong paper; two energy-condition attributions in ENE-2; the ENE-5 gloss of the 2021 critique; the STA-1 and HAZ-2 account of the interior flux; the Winful attribution and Scharnhorst magnitude in LOR-3 and LOR-4; OPERA was corrected, not retracted; LOR-5 split into the WRP family.

Verdict vocabulary for the dossiers: physics proposals **SATISFY**, **VIOLATE**, **DODGE** or are **SILENT** on each entry, or the entry is **N/A**. Fiction **ENGAGES**, has a **RULE**, or is **SILENT**.

## CAU: Causality

| [CAU-1](cau-1.md) | In special relativity any signal faster than light in one inertial frame travels backwards in time in some other inertial frame. Einstein's 1907 review draws the conclusion from the velocity addition theorem: with a signal speed above c one can always choose a frame in which the effect precedes the cause. Tolman states the same paradox; Benford, Book and Newcomb turn it into the two-signal exchange in which a reply arrives before the question was sent, the tachyonic antitelephone | Any FTL signal or vehicle, of any mechanism, unless Lorentz invariance is broken. This is the constraint every loophole must answer | A preferred frame (CAU-4); FTL that only works in one direction or between fixed endpoints; accepting closed timelike curves | Einstein, Jahrbuch der Radioaktivität und Elektronik 4, 411 (1907), §5, pp. 423–424; Tolman, *The Theory of the Relativity of Motion* (1917), §52, pp. 54–55; Benford, Book and Newcomb, Phys. Rev. D 2, 263 (1970) ✓ |
| [CAU-2](cau-2.md) | Chronology protection conjecture: the laws of physics do not allow the appearance of closed timelike curves, because the expectation value of the stress-energy tensor grows very large as timelike curves become almost closed and the back reaction prevents them closing | Any scheme that turns FTL into a time machine, which by CAU-1 and CAU-3 is all of them given two devices | A conjecture, not a theorem; semiclassical, so a full quantum gravity might not obey it | Hawking, Phys. Rev. D 46, 603 (1992) ✓ |
| [CAU-3](cau-3.md) | A wormhole that can be created and maintained for interstellar travel can be converted into a time machine: take one mouth on a relativistic round trip. Everett shows a simple modification of Alcubierre's model, with no extra assumptions, in which two warp bubbles give closed causal loops | Wormholes and warp drives are not merely fast: they are time machines with an assembly step | Chronology protection (CAU-2) if it holds | Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever, Phys. Rev. Lett. 61, 1446 (1988); Everett, Phys. Rev. D 53, 7365 (1996) ✓ |
| [CAU-4](cau-4.md) | The preferred-frame escape. Faster-than-c signals are kinematically compatible with special relativity, which requires an invariant speed rather than a maximum one, and they avoid causal paradox when a soft breaking of Lorentz invariance (a property of the vacuum state, not of the fundamental laws) fixes the FTL speed in one frame. Paradoxes arise only from tachyons whose speed has no fixed value in any frame. The authors argue this for the Scharnhorst case and say statements of general validity are impossible without specifying the propagation | Any FTL scheme that keeps full Lorentz invariance with no fixed FTL speed is a time machine. Any that avoids the time machine has picked a frame, and must say what picks it | This is the escape hatch. Its cost is that special relativity is no longer the whole story for the FTL sector | Liberati, Sonego and Visser, Ann. Phys. 298, 167 (2002), arXiv:gr-qc/0107091 ✓ |
| [CAU-5](cau-5.md) | No-communication theorem: measurements on one half of an entangled pair cannot transmit information to the other half. The statistics of one party's results are unaffected by anything the other party does | Every "entanglement radio" and quantum ansible | None within quantum mechanics. Nonlinear modifications of quantum mechanics would allow signalling and are constrained for that reason (Gisin 1990, Polchinski 1991, not yet verified here) | Eberhard, Nuovo Cimento B 46, 392 (1978); Ghirardi, Rimini and Weber, Lett. ## ENE: Energy

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| [ENE-1](ene-1.md) | The energy conditions. Null (NEC): energy density seen along any light ray is non-negative. Weak (WEC): energy density seen by any observer is non-negative. Averaged null (ANEC): the integral along a complete null geodesic is non-negative. All classical matter obeys them; quantum fields violate the pointwise ones and the ANEC only under restrictions | Not itself a no-go, but the currency every other ENE entry is priced in | Quantum fields (ENE-3, ENE-6) | Hawking and Ellis, *The Large Scale Structure of Space-Time* (1973), §4.3; Visser, *Lorentzian Wormholes* (1995), ch. 12; Curiel, "A Primer on Energy Conditions", in *Towards a Theory of Spacetime Theories*, Einstein Studies 13, pp. 43–104 (2017), arXiv:1405.0403 ✓ (books unverified online) |
| [ENE-2](ene-2.md) | Superluminal travel requires negative energy. Olum: any spacetime allowing a signal to arrive earlier than light through flat space must violate the WEC somewhere along the way, given the generic condition. Visser, Bassett and Liberati: perturbatively around flat space, under the NEC every Shapiro time delay is a delay and never an advance, so effective superluminal travel needs NEC violation ("superluminal censorship"). Gao and Wald: under the NEC plus the null generic condition, a fastest null geodesic between distant points cannot pass through a compact region, so there is no gravitational time advance; the NEC can be weakened to Borde's averaged condition | Every L2 and L3 scheme pays in negative energy; positive-energy warp drives cannot be superluminal | Definitions of "superluminal" that compare against a curved rather than flat background; non-perturbative regimes, which Visser, Bassett and Liberati say their argument does not reach; modified theories of gravity | Olum, Phys. Rev. Lett. 81, 3567 (1998), arXiv:gr-qc/9805003; Visser, Bassett and Liberati, Nucl. Phys. B Proc. Suppl. 88, 267 (2000), arXiv:gr-qc/9810026, with the companion arXiv:gr-qc/9908023; Gao and Wald, Class. Quantum Grav. 17, 4999 (2000), arXiv:gr-qc/0007021 ✓ |
| [ENE-3](ene-3.md) | Quantum inequalities: a quantum field can have negative energy density, but the magnitude times the duration squared is bounded, roughly by the Planck constant. The more negative, the shorter it lasts. Applied to the Alcubierre bubble, the wall must be thinner than a few hundred Planck lengths (about 10² v_b Planck lengths for their sampling choice), and for a 100 m bubble at v_b = 1 the total negative energy is about −6.2 × 10^62 kg, scaling with v_b: roughly ten orders of magnitude beyond the mass of the visible universe. A metre-thick wall would need about a quarter of a solar mass, still unattainable | Any L2 scheme at engineering scale; the bill is not merely large but larger than the universe | Van Den Broeck's pocket geometry (ENE-4); the inequalities are derived for free fields and specific sampling functions, and interacting fields are less constrained | Ford and Roman, Phys. Rev. D 51, 4277 (1995), arXiv:gr-qc/9410043; Ford and Roman, Phys. Rev. D 55, 2082 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9607003; Pfenning and Ford, Class. Quantum Grav. 14, 1743 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9702026 ✓ |
| [ENE-4](ene-4.md) | A bubble whose interior is a 200 m pocket behind a femtometre-sized outer bubble reduces the Alcubierre negative energy from universe scale to about −2 × 10^30 kg, roughly a solar mass, with about 4.9 × 10^30 kg of positive energy alongside. The wall stays within about a hundred Planck lengths and the minimum curvature radius is about ten Planck lengths | Does not remove the negative energy, only its quantity. Structure a few orders of magnitude above the Planck scale is its own bill | None needed; this is itself an escape from ENE-3 | Van Den Broeck, Class. Quantum Grav. 16, 3973 (1999), arXiv:gr-qc/9905084 ✓ |
| [ENE-5](ene-5.md) | Generic warp drives violate the null energy condition. For the general Natário class of warp metrics, the NEC is violated somewhere for any nonzero velocity, superluminal or not, and with it the weak, strong and dominant conditions. The three 2021 positive-energy claims (Lentz, Bobrick and Martire, Fell and Heisenberg) checked energy density only for the co-moving Eulerian observers, which does not establish the WEC; the paper also reports specific calculational errors, including that Lentz's construction does not actually solve the Einstein equations | The positive-energy warp claims of 2021 are not what the press said they were | Metrics outside the Natário class; the subluminal positive-energy constant-velocity shell of 2024 (see WRP-3) | Santiago, Schuster and Visser, Phys. Rev. D 105, 064038 (2022), arXiv:2105.03079 ✓ |
| [ENE-6](ene-6.md) | The Casimir effect is real negative energy density between conducting plates, measured to about 5%. Its magnitude is tiny: energy density −π²ħc/720d⁴, about −4 × 10^-4 J per cubic metre at one micron, falling as the fourth power of the gap | The only negative energy anyone has ever made, and the only one anyone can point to, and it is tens of orders of magnitude short of any bill on this page | None; this is a floor, not a ceiling | Casimir, Proc. K. Ned. Akad. Wet. 51, 793 (1948); Lamoreaux, Phys. Rev. Lett. 78, 5 (1997), erratum 81, 5475 (1998) ✓ |

## CON: Control

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| [CON-1](con-1.md) | A superluminal warp bubble has an event horizon: Hiscock shows a coordinate singularity and horizon form at the wall once the apparent speed exceeds c. Everett and Roman draw the consequence that an observer at the centre of the bubble is causally separated from the outer edge of the wall and can neither create a warp bubble on demand nor control one. Krasnikov shows the leading edge of the bubble matter is spacelike at superluminal speed, so the ship would need tachyons to shape the metric ahead | The pilot's chair is decorative. Control must come from outside, ahead of the bubble | Subluminal warp drives have no horizon; an external agent along the route (CON-2) | Hiscock, Class. Quantum Grav. 14, L183 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9707024; Everett and Roman, Phys. Rev. D 56, 2100 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9702049; Krasnikov, Phys. Rev. D 57, 4760 (1998), arXiv:gr-qc/9511068 ✓ |
| [CON-2](con-2.md) | The bootstrap problem. Krasnikov: the traveller cannot hasten reaching the destination on a first flight, because the metric ahead cannot be created without tachyonic matter; the same effect can be had without tachyons only by placing devices along the way in advance. Coule: the Alcubierre spacetime is impossible to set up without first distributing matter at tachyonic speed, "you need one to make one" | "Just build a warp drive" is not a plan. Warp travel is infrastructure, like a railway, laid at sublight speed | The Krasnikov tube (CON-3), which accepts this and makes the return trip fast. Coule allows that over small distances, where the energy conditions can perhaps be violated, one might open the light cones locally | Krasnikov, Phys. Rev. D 57, 4760 (1998), arXiv:gr-qc/9511068; Coule, Class. Quantum Grav. 15, 2523 (1998) ✓ |
| [CON-3](con-3.md) | The Krasnikov tube: a metric laid down along the outbound sublight journey that makes an arbitrarily long round trip take an arbitrarily short time by the traveller's clock and the home clock alike. Everett and Roman show the tube, like warp bubbles and traversable wormholes, needs unphysically thin layers of negative energy density and large total negative energies, and that a time machine can be built from two non-overlapping tubes | The honest L2 scheme, and it still pays the ENE-3 bill and the CAU-3 bill | None beyond those of ENE-3 | Krasnikov, Phys. Rev. D 57, 4760 (1998); Everett and Roman, "A superluminal subway: The Krasnikov tube", Phys. Rev. D 56, 2100 (1997), arXiv:gr-qc/9702049 ✓ |

## STA: Stability

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| [STA-1](sta-1.md) | Semiclassical instability of warp bubbles. Hiscock: the renormalised stress-energy of a quantised field diverges on the event horizon that forms when the apparent speed exceeds c, whatever the wall profile (a two-dimensional reduction). Finazzi, Liberati and Barceló: in a dynamically formed superluminal bubble the renormalised stress-energy grows exponentially in time close to and on the front wall, on a timescale set by the wall thickness, and every one of these problems disappears while the bubble stays subluminal, since no horizons form | A superluminal bubble is not a stable object even if you could build it | Subluminal bubbles are stable in this analysis; both calculations are 1+1 dimensional; full quantum gravity unknown | Hiscock, Class. | [STA-2](sta-2.md) | A traversable wormhole throat must be held open by matter whose radial tension exceeds its mass-energy density, which no known material satisfies and which violates the energy conditions: Morris and Thorne's "exotic matter". Visser's thin-shell examples confine all of it to a delta-function layer at the throat, and the weak energy condition is violated there | Every L3 wormhole pays in negative energy, and the amount scales with throat radius | None classical; quantum fields at the throat, subject to ENE-3 | Morris and Thorne, Am. J. Phys. 56, 395 (1988); Visser, Phys. Rev. D 39, 3182 (1989), arXiv:0809.0907 ✓ |
| [STA-3](sta-3.md) | Divergence at the chronology horizon: when a wormhole is converted into a time machine, the renormalised stress-energy of quantum fields diverges as one approaches the Cauchy horizon. Kim and Thorne find the divergence extremely weak and propose that quantum gravity cuts it off, which would allow closed timelike curves; Hawking conjectures the opposite | The time-machine step in CAU-3 may be self-destroying | The Kim and Thorne cutoff, unresolved | Kim and Thorne, Phys. Rev. D 43, 3929 (1991); Hawking, Phys. Rev. D 46, 603 (1992) ✓ |

## LOR: Kinematics

| ID | Result | What it forbids or costs | Escape hatches | Citation |
|----|--------|--------------------------|----------------|----------|
| [LOR-1](lor-1.md) | A massive body cannot be accelerated to c: its kinetic energy grows without bound as v approaches c, so velocities greater than light have no possibility of existence for anything that starts below it | Every L1 scheme that proposes to push harder | Never having been below c (tachyons, LOR-2); changing the geometry instead of the velocity (L2, L3) | Einstein, Ann. Phys. 322, 891 (1905), §10; Rindler, *Relativity: Special, General, and Cosmological* (2006), ch. 6 ✓ (Rindler chapter ⚠ v1) |
| [LOR-2](lor-2.md) | Tachyons. Bilaniuk, Deshpande and Sudarshan re-examine faster-than-light particles within classical special relativity: such a particle has spacelike four-momentum, is always faster than light and can never slow below it. Feinberg builds a Lorentz-invariant field theory for them. Aharonov, Komar and Susskind show that causal superluminal group velocities arise as small oscillations about an unstable configuration, so the field-theory tachyon is an instability, not a courier. Tachyonic particles would in any case give the antitelephone (CAU-1) | L1 by particle physics is closed: the tachyon is an instability, and if it were a particle it would be a time machine | None found in nearly sixty years of searching; Lorentz-violating frameworks | Bilaniuk, Deshpande and Sudarshan, Am. J. Phys. 30, 718 (1962); Feinberg, Phys. Rev. 159, 1089 (1967); Aharonov, Komar and Susskind, Phys. Rev. 182, 1400 (1969) ✓ |
| [LOR-3](lor-3.md) | Phase velocity and group velocity may exceed c; the front velocity of a signal cannot. Sommerfeld and Brillouin showed the wavefront in any causal medium travels at exactly c. Tunnelling "superluminality" (the Hartman effect, the Nimtz experiments, the single-photon measurement of Steinberg, Kwiat and Chiao at 1.7c) is not a genuine signal velocity: Winful shows the group delay in tunnelling is a lifetime of stored energy, not a transit time. The gain-assisted pulses of Wang, Kuzmich and Dogariu are classical interference between frequency components in anomalous dispersion. Neither is a signal beating its own front | Every L5 claim built on a group velocity or a tunnelling time | None; this is settled | Brillouin, *Wave Propagation and Group Velocity* (1960); Steinberg, Kwiat and Chiao, Phys. Rev. Lett. 71, 708 (1993); Wang, Kuzmich and Dogariu, Nature 406, 277 (2000); Winful, Phys. Rep. 436, 1 (2006) ✓ |
| [LOR-4](lor-4.md) | The Scharnhorst effect: light propagating perpendicular to Casimir plates has a phase velocity exceeding c by a fraction of order 10^-32 for micron plates, falling as the fourth power of the gap, and too small by many orders of magnitude to measure. Liberati, Sonego and Visser show the effect is benign and constrained so as not to lead to causality violation. The OPERA superluminal-neutrino anomaly of 2011 was traced to a mis-seated optical fibre connection and an oscillator frequency offset; the corrected result, consistent with c, is the published paper. Nothing was retracted | The one "measured" superluminal velocity in the literature was an instrument, and the one calculated is unmeasurable and causally harmless | None | Scharnhorst, Phys. Lett. B 236, 354 (1990), erratum 787, 204 (2018); Barton, Phys. Lett. B 237, 559 (1990); Liberati, Sonego and Visser, Ann. ## Reading the register

Three entries do almost all the work. CAU-1 says any FTL is a message to the past unless something breaks Lorentz invariance. ENE-2 says any geometric FTL costs negative energy. CON-2 says the negative energy has to be laid along the route in advance at sublight speed. A proposal that answers those three has answered the field; the rest of the register is what it costs once it has.

The register is deliberately physics-internal. It does not score plausibility, engineering readiness or taste. Those live in the dossiers.
