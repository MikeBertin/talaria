Pick a drive, pick a frame

Every faster-than-light drive in the catalogue, from warp bubbles to jump gates to ansibles, can carry a message out and a reply back. Choose one, set how fast the other observer is moving and see whether the reply lands before you sent. Then read what the story itself says about that.

Controls

Diagram is a spacetime diagram: position across, time up, light at 45°. The other observer starts one light-unit away, so one time unit is the time light takes to cross that gap. Everything above the dashed line happened after you sent. If the return leg lands below it, it arrived before you sent.

Things to notice

An instant jump needs almost no motion to break

Pick any shortcut, hyperspace or ansible drive and nudge the other observer's speed to 0.05c. The return leg already lands before you sent. The faster the drive, the lower the speed that breaks it, all the way down to zero.

The threshold

Warp and hyperfast ships get a speed dial

Pick a warp drive or a faster-in-flat-space ship and the speed slider wakes up. At 1.1c the other observer needs nearly 0.995c to cause trouble. At 20c, a tenth of light speed does it.

Why the dial matters

The sublight ships cannot build the diagram

Pick a generation ship, a ramjet or a hibernation liner. Slide the other observer's speed anywhere you like. The return leg always lands after you sent, and if they outrun your message it never reaches them at all.

What is invariant

What is going on

The register's first causality entry, CAU-1, says that any signal faster than light in one frame runs backwards in time in some other frame, and that two such signals between people in relative motion deliver the answer before the question. That is true of any mechanism at all. A warp bubble that moves the space, a wormhole that shortens the path, a jump through hyperspace and an ansible that sends only the message all count as the same kind of signal once they beat light between two points. This page treats each drive as exactly that signal and draws the two-leg exchange from the antitelephone demo.

The only way out that keeps the drive is CAU-4, the preferred-frame escape: if "instant" means instant in one special frame for everyone, the two legs no longer add up. The cost is that special relativity is no longer the whole story for the FTL sector, and the story has to say what picks the frame. The catalogue marks each fictional drive on what it does about all this. Most say nothing. A few own it and build the plot on it. A few dodge it with a stated rule. The sublight ships, kept as a control group, never owed the bill in the first place.
