The idea

Every faster-than-light scheme leans on one of six moves. Go faster in the same space. Move the space instead of the ship. Take a shortcut through a tunnel or a fold. Leave the manifold for some other space with laxer rules. Send the message rather than the ship. Or, more often than you would think, not beat light at all and get filed here by habit.

Against those sits the register: the theorems, inequalities and instabilities that put a price on each move. Three of them do most of the work. Any signal faster than light is a message to somebody's past unless something breaks Lorentz invariance. Any geometric shortcut needs negative energy. And the negative energy has to be laid along the route in advance, at sublight speed, which makes a warp drive a railway rather than a car.

Real proposals are stated in their strongest form and then scored: satisfies, violates, dodges or silent, against every entry. Fiction cannot violate a theorem, so it is graded on what it does with the physics it chose to invoke: does the story engage with the cost, follow a rule that happens to answer it, or never notice? The join between the two is the point.
