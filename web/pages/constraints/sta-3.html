STA-3: Divergence at the chronology horizon

The time-machine step in CAU-3 may be self-destroying.

Kind: constraint · Family: STA (Stability) · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The result

Divergence at the chronology horizon: when a wormhole is converted into a time machine, the renormalised stress-energy of quantum fields diverges as one approaches the Cauchy horizon. Kim and Thorne find the divergence extremely weak and propose that quantum gravity cuts it off, which would allow closed timelike curves; Hawking conjectures the opposite

What it forbids or costs

The time-machine step in CAU-3 may be self-destroying

Escape hatches

The Kim and Thorne cutoff, unresolved

Who it bites

Physics proposals

  • VIOLATES: Gott's cosmic-string time machine (1991). Grant computes the renormalised stress-energy on the chronology horizon and polarised hypersurfaces of the Gott spacetime and finds it diverges strongly enough for order-one back reaction before the Planck scale; the machine requires that divergence not to matter.
  • VIOLATES: Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever: the wormhole time machine (1988). The construction creates a compactly generated chronology horizon at which the renormalised stress-energy of quantum fields diverges (Kim and Thorne); the machine survives only if that divergence is cut off, which the register records as unresolved.
  • VIOLATES: Passage through the Kerr interior (Carter 1968). The route runs through a Cauchy horizon at which the classical stress-energy (Poisson–Israel mass inflation) and the renormalised quantum stress-energy (Zilberman et al. 2022) both diverge; this is the same Cauchy-horizon divergence STA-3 records for wormhole time machines, and here there is no proposed cutoff.
  • SATISFIES: Gao, Jafferis and Wall: traversable wormholes via a double-trace deformation (2017). No chronology horizon forms because no time machine can be built from this wormhole.
  • SATISFIES: Maldacena, Milekhin and Popov: traversable wormholes in four dimensions (2018), and the humanly traversable sequel (2021). No chronology horizon forms because no time machine can be built from a long wormhole.
  • SILENT: Alcubierre 1994: the warp drive metric. The chronology-horizon divergence would arise only at the two-bubble time machine of CAU-3, which the letter does not construct; whether the Kim and Thorne cutoff or Hawking's divergence wins there is unsettled and uncomputed for warp bubbles.
  • SILENT: Everett and Roman 1997: the superluminal subway. The two-tube machine has a chronology horizon whose semiclassical behaviour the paper does not compute; the Kim and Thorne versus Hawking question is unsettled there.
  • SILENT: Krasnikov 1998: the tube laid on the way out. The two-tube time machine would have a chronology horizon; Krasnikov discusses the divergence of the sampled energy density near the Cauchy horizon of a wormhole time machine as a feature to exploit, and whether Hawking's or Kim and Thorne's account holds there is unsettled.
  • SILENT: Morris–Thorne traversable wormhole (1988). The 1988 paper predates the time-machine conversion; the chronology-horizon divergence bears once a mouth is moved, and the literature (Kim and Thorne versus Hawking) has not settled whether it destroys the machine.
  • SILENT: Natário 2002: warp drive with zero expansion. The chronology horizon would appear only in the two-bubble time machine, which is not constructed here and whose semiclassical fate is unsettled.
  • SILENT: Van Den Broeck 1999: the pocket geometry. Only the two-pocket time machine would raise a chronology horizon; not constructed or analysed.
  • SILENT: Visser's thin-shell wormholes (1989). The paper names causal constraints as open and does not compute the chronology-horizon divergence; the literature has not settled whether the Kim–Thorne cutoff saves the converted machine.
  • SILENT: Warp drives in modified gravity. The two-bubble chronology horizon is neither constructed nor analysed in these theories.
