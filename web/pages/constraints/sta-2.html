STA-2: The throat needs exotic matter

Every L3 wormhole pays in negative energy, and the amount scales with throat radius.

Kind: constraint · Family: STA (Stability) · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The result

A traversable wormhole throat must be held open by matter whose radial tension exceeds its mass-energy density, which no known material satisfies and which violates the energy conditions: Morris and Thorne's "exotic matter". Visser's thin-shell examples confine all of it to a delta-function layer at the throat, and the weak energy condition is violated there

What it forbids or costs

Every L3 wormhole pays in negative energy, and the amount scales with throat radius

Escape hatches

None classical; quantum fields at the throat, subject to ENE-3

Who it bites

Physics proposals

  • VIOLATES: Gao, Jafferis and Wall: traversable wormholes via a double-trace deformation (2017). The throat is opened by matter with negative averaged null energy, as STA-2 says every traversable throat must be; here the exotic ingredient is a quantum field under a boundary coupling rather than classical exotic matter.
  • VIOLATES: Maldacena, Milekhin and Popov: traversable wormholes in four dimensions (2018), and the humanly traversable sequel (2021). The throat is held open by negative energy, as STA-2 says every traversable throat must be; here it is Casimir energy of Standard Model or dark-sector fermions rather than exotic matter, and the amount scales with the flux and the length.
  • VIOLATES: Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever: the wormhole time machine (1988). The throat is held open by exotic matter whose tension exceeds its density, as STA-2 states; the time machine inherits the throat unchanged.
  • VIOLATES: Morris–Thorne traversable wormhole (1988). This is the source paper for STA-2: the throat must be held open by matter with $\tau_0 > \rho_0 c^2$, which violates the energy conditions, and the tension scales as $1/r_0^2$.
  • VIOLATES: Visser's thin-shell wormholes (1989). This is the paper STA-2 cites for the delta-function layer: all exotic matter sits on the throat, the weak energy condition is violated there, and the total scales with throat size.
  • SATISFIES: Einstein–Rosen bridge (1935). The throat has no exotic matter and, as STA-2 predicts for such a throat, it is not traversable: it pinches off.
  • SATISFIES: ER = EPR (Maldacena and Susskind 2013). The bridge contains no exotic matter and, as STA-2 predicts, is not traversable; the conjecture depends on this remaining true.
  • SATISFIES: Wheeler's geons and quantum foam (1955–1962). These throats contain no exotic matter, and as STA-2 predicts they are not traversable: Schwarzschild pinches off, Reissner–Nordström pulsates behind horizons.
  • SILENT: The Mach effect thruster. The book offers the wormhole term as the exotic matter STA-2 requires; no published calculation shows a throat held open by it, and the thruster papers do not address the throat

Fictional drives

  • SILENT: 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968): the Star Gate. What holds the gate open is never described; the monolith is a slab and the passage is a corridor, with no throat matter or tension mentioned.
  • SILENT: Banks's Culture: the energy grid, infraspace and ultraspace (1987 onward). Displacement throats would need the register's exotic matter; the novels state a range that falls with mass and a one-in-sixty-one-million failure, and nothing about what holds the throat.
  • SILENT: Battlestar Galactica: the FTL jump (2004 series). A jump that connects two points is a wormhole with the throat unstated; nothing is said of what opens or holds it.
  • SILENT: Commonwealth: CST wormholes with trains through them (Hamilton 2004). What threads a gateway's throat is never described; trains simply pass through.
  • SILENT: Contact: the Machine and the tunnel system (Sagan 1985, Zemeckis 1997). The throat must be threaded by matter whose tension exceeds its energy density; this is the Morris and Thorne result the novel was the occasion for, and the text never describes what holds the tunnel open.
  • SILENT: Dune: the Guild navigators and folding space (Herbert 1965). A fold that joins two points is a wormhole with the throat unstated; what holds it open is never described.
  • SILENT: Elite Dangerous: the frame shift drive (Frontier Developments, 2014). If witch-space is a corridor with an aperture, something holds it open; the game does not say what.
  • SILENT: EVE Online: the warp drive (CCP Games, 2003). Stargates hold something open and the lore declines to say what.
  • SILENT: Event Horizon (1997): the gravity drive. A gateway held open long enough for a ship to pass is a throat, and the film names nothing that holds it. The drive core is rings around a containment sphere; what it contains is never said.
  • SILENT: Farscape (1999): wormholes, and starburst as a separate thing. What holds a wormhole open is never described; wormholes open, are navigated and close with no throat matter or tension mentioned.
  • SILENT: Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2 (2017): jump points. What holds an access point open is never described; the portals simply exist and are used.
  • SILENT: Halo: slipspace and the Shaw-Fujikawa translight engine (2001 onward). A rupture between spaces held open long enough for a ship to pass is a throat, and the register says a throat needs matter whose tension exceeds its density; the franchise opens it with microscopic black holes that evaporate in nanoseconds and never says what holds it after that.
  • SILENT: Homeworld: hyperspace cores and the Far Jumpers. The gates are held open by nothing the games name; a throat's exotic matter is never mentioned. Applies through the gates and is never addressed.
  • SILENT: Hyperion: the farcasters (Simmons 1989). What holds a doorway open is never described beyond the Core's word "singularity"; the frame of a farcaster is where a thin-shell wormhole would put its exotic matter and the novels say nothing about it.
  • SILENT: Interstellar: the wormhole near Saturn (2014). The exotic matter at the throat is the book's subject, not the film's; on screen the mouth is a sphere of lensed starlight and nothing is said of what threads it.
  • SILENT: Mass Effect: the mass relays (BioWare 2007 onward). What holds a corridor open is never described beyond the words "mass-free"; the throat has no stated matter.
  • SILENT: No Man's Sky: the hyperdrive, warp cells and range. The portals are held open by nothing the game names; a throat's exotic matter is never mentioned. Applies through the portals and is never addressed.
  • SILENT: Outer Wilds (2019): Nomai warp cores. What holds a warp throat open is never described; the cores are black boxes with a pairing rule. Brittle Hollow's natural hole is traversable without comment.
  • SILENT: Star Citizen: quantum drive and jump points. What holds a jump point open is never described; the lore says they are natural and studied for centuries without understanding, which is an admission of silence.
  • SILENT: Star Trek: transwarp, quantum slipstream and the other beyond-warp drives. Conduit apertures and the coaxial fold are throats held open by something; what holds them and whether it is exotic is never stated.
  • SILENT: Starfield (2023): the grav drive. What holds a jump open is never described; the drive spools, flashes and is done.
  • SILENT: Stargate: the gate network (1994 film, SG-1 1997 onward). What threads the throat is never described; the event horizon is a pool of light and the gate a ring of naquadah.
  • SILENT: Starman Jones: the congruencies (Heinlein 1953). The fold is two flat surfaces that fit; what holds the join open is never described.
  • SILENT: Super Dimension Fortress Macross (1982): the space fold. What holds a fold open long enough for a warship and an island to pass is never described; the fold system is a black box that is gone after one use.
  • SILENT: The Alderson drive (Niven and Pournelle 1974). The ship is not described as passing through a throat; the tramline is a line, and what holds a connection open is never described.
  • SILENT: The Expanse: the ring gates and the slow zone (Corey 2013 onward). What threads the throat of a ring is never described.
  • SILENT: The Forever War: collapsar jumps (Haldeman 1974). The Stargate collapsar is a frozen star with a surface, not a throat threaded by exotic matter; what keeps the passage open is never described because in 1974 the question had not been asked.
  • SILENT: Thor (2011): the Bifrost. What holds the bridge open is Asgard's mechanism, unexplained; no throat matter or tension is ever described.
  • SILENT: Weber's Honorverse: hyper bands and Warshawski sails (1993 onward). Junction throats would need the register's exotic matter; the series never says what holds a junction open.
  • SILENT: Wing Commander (1990) and the 1999 film: jump points. What holds a point open is never described; points are permanent map features in the game and gravitational anomalies in the film, with nothing said about either's stability.
  • ENGAGES: Cowboy Bebop (1998): the astral gates. "Gateway Shuffle": a gate shut down mid-transit strands the ship in the tunnel and shows only an image of it emerging. The series never says "throat", but it depicts what a throat that stops being held open does to a traveller, and the 2022 accident is a gate failing with nothing to contain the failure.
  • RULE: Stellaris: warp, hyperlane and wormhole (Paradox Development Studio, 2016). A wormhole must be held open by a station, or stabilised by a technology before a natural one can be used: a stated rule that answers the throat-support constraint without saying what supports it.
