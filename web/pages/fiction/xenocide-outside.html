Xenocide: travel through the Outside

Orson Scott Card's 1991 answer to his own instantaneous ansible: a ship whose every particle is held in one mind is pushed out of the universe and back in somewhere else, and whatever else that mind holds comes back with it.

Kind: fiction · Loophole: L4 · Notability: N3 · Coherence: F3 · Causality: C-silent · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The drive as depicted

Xenocide (Legend, 1991; Tor, 1992) is the third Ender novel, and for most of its length the universe has the physics of the first two: starships that travel close to light speed and skip decades of the world's time, and the ansible, a device for instantaneous communication across any distance [HIGH] S3. The ansible was defined in Ender's Game (1985) as the Philotic Parallax Instantaneous Communicator, with Colonel Graff remarking that somebody dredged the name out of an old book; its stated basis is that the quarks in a pi meson can be separated by any distance and remain joined by philotic rays along which change is instantaneous [HIGH] S3. That device has its own dossier in the L5 catalogue. Xenocide builds the starship on the same particle.

The theory is worked out on Lusitania by the physicist Grego Ribeira with his brother Olhado, across the chapters "Grego's War", "Free Will" and "Life and Death", and tested in "Voyage" [MED] S1. Philotes, in the novel's account, are the true fundamental constituents of matter: they have no mass and no position, only connections, and everything from a quark to a planet is a pattern of philotic twining. Each living pattern is organised by one philote the novel calls its aiúa, a term Card takes from Sanskrit for the self [MED] S1. Where a philote is not twined into anything it exists Outside, a place with no space and no time in which every philote is equally distant from every other [MED] S1. Olhado's insight is that a starship is a pattern, and if some aiúa could hold the whole pattern of the ship and its passengers at once, down to the state of every particle, the ship could be taken Outside, where location does not exist, and brought back Inside at any chosen point, with no time passing [MED] S1. The only mind that could hold such a pattern is Jane, the intelligence that lives in the ansible network's computers, and the novel is explicit that the task takes all of her capacity [HIGH] S3.

The test flight in "Voyage" is a bare room of a ship with Ender, Miro and Ela aboard; Jane takes it Outside and back to a point in orbit, the trip is instantaneous and it works [HIGH] S3. The side effect is the novel's real subject. Outside, whatever a mind holds strongly enough is made: Miro, crippled in Speaker for the Dead, holds the pattern of his healthy body and steps back into it; Ender, holding the two people who defined his childhood, brings back a thirteen-year-old copy of his sister Valentine and a young copy of his brother Peter, both with his own aiúa animating them [HIGH] S3. Ela brings back the virus she has designed on paper. The book ends with the drive proven and its cost unpaid.

Children of the Mind (1996) makes the cost the plot: Starways Congress orders the ansible network shut down to kill Jane, which would also end the only means of instantaneous travel, and Jane survives by moving her aiúa into the body of the young Valentine; the drive continues to work only through her [MED] S3.

Origin and lineage

Card wrote Xenocide as the third of what became four Ender novels; it was nominated for the Hugo and Locus awards in 1992 [HIGH] S3. Card has said in print that he took the word "ansible" from Le Guin, needing the same rule set she used, instantaneous communication in a universe of slower-than-light ships [HIGH] S1 (Hatrack River, "OSC Answers Questions", readable directly over plain http); the lineage page for the ansible belongs to the L5 dossiers (Le Guin's ansible and Card's philotic ansible), and the Outside is Card's own extension of it, unattested elsewhere. Philotes resemble entangled particles in that separated parts remain correlated without a signal passing between them, and the novel's "philotic ray" plays the role a popular account gives to a nonlocal link, but Card names no physics paper and this dossier asserts no source; the resemblance is recorded, not the influence. No physicist has been found citing the Outside.

Lineage: Le Guin's ansible goes out into the field.

Which loophole, and why

L4, leave the manifold, and the novel is unusual in saying so directly: the ship goes Outside, a place that is not in space or time, and comes back Inside somewhere else. The transit is not motion (L1), not a moving region of space (L2) and not a tunnel between two points in our spacetime (L3); the Outside has no points. The novel's own metaphysics makes the Outside prior to space rather than beside it, which is a stronger claim than most hyperspace fiction makes.

The physics cousins are three. Entanglement signalling and the no-communication theorem is the cousin of the philote itself: measuring one half of an entangled pair changes nothing the other half can see, and every scheme to make a correlation carry a message has failed at the theorem; its standing is K0 and its bill B-new and B-caus. The Outside drive rests on philotic connection carrying not only messages but a whole ship, so the theorem is the first thing it would meet. ER = EPR is the conjecture that every entangled pair is joined by a bridge, and the finding that the bridge carries nothing through it; that is the nearest physics to "every philote is connected to every other" and it forbids exactly the traversal the novel makes. For the space part, the Kaluza-Klein hyperspace reading is the negative result that no extra dimension is a place with laxer rules. The register entries are CAU-5 and MAN-1. The sublight baseline the series keeps for its ordinary ships is the relativistic rocket.

The rules it keeps

The Coherence mark is F3, acknowledges, because the work states its limitations and applies them consistently across two novels, and because it keeps a real constraint for everything except the new drive. The stated limitations are these. Only a mind that holds the whole pattern can take a ship Outside, and only Jane, running on the whole ansible network, can hold one; the drive is therefore not a technology that can be built, only a person who can be asked. The trip brings back whatever the passengers hold in their minds, which is a hazard the characters know and a cost the sequel enforces. The ship is a bare box because the drive does not need anything else. When the network is shut down the drive stops. None of these is bent for the plot; the sequel's crisis is built from them.

The real constraint the series keeps is time dilation. Ender is three thousand years old by the calendar and thirty-odd by his own clock because the ships are sublight vessels, the sequence of the novels depends on it, and the ansible's instantaneity is used precisely to make that gap bite. The mark stops at F3 rather than F4 because the new drive itself engages no real constraint: the Outside is exempted from physics by definition, and what it costs is a fictional currency.

What it ignores

The register's questions are not asked of the Outside. The novel does not say what energy the transit takes (none, apparently, since Jane's effort is computational), what happens to the light cones of the departure and arrival points, or why a return Inside is at a chosen point when the Outside has no points to choose from. The creation side effect is explored as a moral problem and not as a conservation problem.

Causality is C-silent, and the argument for silent rather than dodges is this. The Ender universe runs on a single galactic "now": the ansible is instantaneous for everyone and the novels never find a pair of observers who disagree about the order of ansible messages, so the world has an unexamined preferred frame in which "instantaneous" is defined. If the novel stated that frame, the mark would be C-dodges. It never does. Xenocide also makes the Outside timeless, which removes the problem from the Outside without removing it from the Inside, where the ship reappears at a definite time on a definite clock nobody names. Last checked 2026-09-12 across Xenocide and Children of the Mind: the antitelephone is never raised for the ansible or for the ship.
