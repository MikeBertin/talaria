Stellaris: warp, hyperlane and wormhole (Paradox Development Studio, 2016)

A strategy game that offered its players a choice of loophole at the start, one each from L2, L4 and L3, and then in 2018 took two of them away.

Kind: fiction · Loophole: L2 · Notability: N3 · Coherence: F2 · Causality: C-silent · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The drive as depicted

Stellaris at release (version 1.0, May 2016) made faster-than-light travel a choice each empire fixed at creation, and the game's Dev Diary #4, "Means of Travel" (October 2015), is the designers' own definition of the three options [HIGH] S3 (developer statement; the ModDB repost dated April 2016 was the copy read). Warp needs a warp drive on every ship, costly and power-hungry, and allows travel to any system within range in any direction; a journey beyond range is a sequence of jumps, each of which strains the drive and imposes a cooldown; fleets must jump from the edge of a system "to lessen the gravitational pull of the local star", and warp is the slowest of the three, so an arriving fleet's entry point is predictable and can be ambushed [HIGH] S3. Wormhole travel dispenses with the drive: a Wormhole Station on the edge of a system opens a temporary wormhole through which a fleet is moved instantly to a distant system; the station generates one wormhole at a time, a larger fleet takes longer to prepare, the wormhole allows two-way travel and collapses almost at once, and an empire's reach is its network of stations, which can be disabled by raiders [HIGH] S3. Hyperdrive uses a hidden network of hyperlanes connecting systems, visible only to those who can see it, along which ships travel fast but only along the lanes; voids without systems block movement, lane junctions are chokepoints, and because the lanes exist in subspace a fleet can enter one from anywhere in a system after a long charge-up [HIGH] S3. The diary closes with the warning that as more empires bend the laws of physics to move larger fleets, something or someone may notice, which in play becomes the Unbidden, extradimensional invaders drawn by jump-drive use [HIGH] S3 for the diary's sentence, [MED] S3 for the in-game trigger.

Patch 2.0 "Cherryh", released 22 February 2018, removed the choice. The official patch notes say that every empire now starts with the hyperdrive and that wormhole travel and warp travel are no longer available as starting FTL types; in their place pairs of natural wormholes are scattered across the galaxy and can be traversed once a Wormhole Stabilization technology is researched, ancient Gateways form a network of instant travel once activated and can later be built, and Jump Drives allow near-instantaneous point-to-point jumps that ignore hyperlanes at the cost of a long cooldown and a significant debuff to the fleet; FTL Inhibitors at starbases and fortresses hold hostile fleets to the lane they entered by [HIGH] S1 for the patch notes as reproduced on the official wiki. The current game (version 4.x) keeps that structure: hyperlanes with a charge-up at the entry point, jump drives with a roughly two-hundred-day recharge and combat penalties, natural wormholes and gateways as bypasses, quantum catapults and the L-Gate network opened through a single terminal system [HIGH] S3.

Origin and lineage

Stellaris is a grand-strategy game from Paradox Development Studio, directed at launch by Henrik Fåhraeus, released on 9 May 2016 [MED] S3. Dev Diary #4 credits the design to the studio and is signed by the community; Dev Diary #92, "FTL Rework and Galactic Terrain" (late 2017), announced the 2.0 change and its reasoning, and could not be opened for this dossier because the forum blocks automated readers [LOW] S3. The publicly reported rationale, that a single travel system allows terrain, chokepoints and defensible borders and that three parallel systems could not be balanced against each other, is consistent with the patch notes' addition of inhibitors and gateways [MED] S3.

The three modes are the three great fictional loopholes named as menu items, and the game is in this catalogue for that reason: it is the cleanest example of a work treating L2, L3 and L4 as interchangeable design choices with no physical difference between them, only a strategic one. No real proposal is named. The warp mode's rule about jumping from the edge of a gravity well is the one place the design touches physics, and it is a gameplay balance rule with a physical excuse. Nothing in physics descends from Stellaris.

Which loophole, and why

L2 in the metadata for the warp mode, which is the mode the catalogue filed the game under and the one that carries the register's name; the game as it now exists is L4 first, since every empire begins with hyperlanes, and a case can be made for revising the entry. The three modes are priced separately.

Warp is a drive on the ship that moves the fleet through ordinary space to any system within range, with a charge-up and a cooldown: the physics cousin is Alcubierre 1994, whose bill is negative energy of order $10^{62}$ kg for a ship-sized bubble under the quantum inequalities (ENE-3), a horizon that makes the bubble uncontrollable from inside (CON-1), a route laid in advance (CON-2) and a time machine from two bubbles (CAU-3). The game's rule that warp must begin at the edge of a gravity well has no register counterpart; nothing in the Alcubierre literature makes a bubble harder to form near a star.

Hyperlanes are lanes in subspace along which ships move fast, a fixed network the ship enters and leaves: L4, priced against Kaluza-Klein hyperspace and brane-bulk shortcuts, whose bill is MAN-1: extra dimensions in physics are compact or warped and share the ordinary light cone, so there is no subspace with its own lanes. The one real bulk shortcut is of order $10^{-58}$.

Wormholes, generated by a station or found in natural pairs, are L3, priced against Morris and Thorne 1988: a throat held open by exotic matter whose quantity scales with the throat radius (STA-2), tidal limits that force the throat to hundreds of kilometres for a human traveller (HAZ-3), and topological censorship forbidding any shortcut without achronal ANEC violation (ENE-7). Jump drives after 2.0 are L3 without a fixed endpoint and are priced the same way with no throat to point at.

The rules it keeps

Stellaris is a rules engine and keeps every rule it states, for every empire, until a patch changes it.

  • Range, charge-up and cooldown. Warp had range and a post-jump cooldown; hyperlanes have a charge-up at the entry point; jump drives have a long recharge and a combat debuff [HIGH] S3.
  • Geometry. Warp from the system edge; wormholes from a station; hyperlanes from anywhere but only along lanes; gateways only from gateways [HIGH] S3.
  • Infrastructure. Wormhole stations, gateways and the L-Gate terminal are things that are built, activated, defended and lost [HIGH] S1.
  • Denial. FTL Inhibitors hold fleets to their entry lane, which is the 2.0 patch's replacement for the ambush geometry of warp and the network fragility of wormholes [HIGH] S1.
  • A cost to the fabric. Jump-drive use can attract the Unbidden, extradimensional invaders, which is the diary's warning made into a game event [MED] S3.

Internally consistent rules with no physical content, applied by software: F2. The gravity-well rule and the Unbidden are the closest the game comes to a physical cost and both are balance mechanisms with a fictional gloss.

What it ignores

Time. Stellaris has one galactic calendar, ticking in days, and every mode of travel takes a number of those days and lands on the same calendar for every empire. No event, technology, diary or patch note has considered what a faster-than-light jump does to the order of events, and the game's few time-travel flavours (an anomaly here, an event chain there) are unconnected to the drives. There is no stated rule that prevents the antitelephone, only a single clock in the game engine. C-silent, dated 2026-09-12 across versions 1.0 to 4.x.
