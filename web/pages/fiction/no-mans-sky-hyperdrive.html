No Man's Sky: the hyperdrive, warp cells and range

Hello Games' 2016 galaxy of eighteen quintillion planets is crossed by a ship-mounted hyperdrive with a range in light-years, a fuel cell per warp and a drive upgrade for each colour of star.

Kind: fiction · Loophole: L4 · Notability: N3 · Coherence: F2 · Causality: C-silent · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The drive as depicted

No Man's Sky was released by Hello Games on 9 August 2016 and has been rebuilt by free updates since; the drive's rules below are those of the current game, with the launch version noted where it differed [HIGH] S3. The player begins on a planet with a crashed ship and no hyperdrive, and the opening mission has them build one from a blueprint and fuel it before the first warp out of the starting system [MED] S3. The drive is a ship technology occupying an inventory slot, with a stated range in light-years shown on the galactic map: about a hundred light-years for a bare drive, rising with installed upgrade modules to several thousand for a well-fitted explorer [MED] S3. Only systems within range can be selected as a destination; the map draws the reachable ones.

Fuel is the warp cell, crafted from antimatter and a housing, and one cell is consumed per warp, a fifth of the drive's charge; the drive can be pre-charged with five, and a later item, the warp hypercore, fills it in one [MED] S3. Star colour is a gate. A bare hyperdrive reaches only yellow stars; the Cadmium Drive adds red, the Emeril Drive green and the Indium Drive blue, each requiring the previous, so the galaxy's colour classes are a progression the player unlocks [HIGH] S3. The warp itself is a tunnel of streaked light through which the ship flies for a few seconds before dropping into the destination system near its star, with the option since later updates of being interrupted by a pirate ambush mid-warp [MED] S3. Freighters have their own hyperdrive with its own range, and a player's fleet warps with them [MED] S3.

Two other means of crossing the galaxy sit alongside the drive and give it its shape. Black holes, found through the Atlas storyline, throw the ship a large distance toward the galactic centre at the cost of damaged ship components, and portals on planet surfaces connect any two portals by a twelve-glyph address; reaching the galactic centre itself, by successive warps, sends the player to the next galaxy in the sequence of 256 [MED] S3. The drive is the slow, honest tool among these, the one with a number attached.

Origin and lineage

Hello Games, led by Sean Murray, announced the game in 2013 and shipped it in 2016 to a reception dominated by the gap between the promise and the launch build; the free updates that followed (Foundation, Pathfinder, Atlas Rises, NEXT and onward) added the freighter drives, the colour-class drives and the black holes and portals described above [HIGH] S3. The hyperdrive is the plain hyperdrive of games and television since Star Wars, and the game's lore, told through the Atlas and the Travellers, is about simulation and memory rather than about propulsion; no real proposal is named and none fed the drive, and this dossier asserts no influence. The game's colour-coded star classes borrow loosely from the astronomical spectral sequence, red for cool and blue for hot, without claiming the physics.

Which loophole, and why

L4, leave the manifold. The ship enters a tunnel that is not the space it left and emerges in another system; range and fuel limit which systems, and nothing about the transit is motion through our space. It is not a speed (L1) and not a carried bubble (L2). The game's two L3 devices, black holes and portals, are shortcuts with two ends in the world and are the game's own reminder of the difference. The hyperdrive is filed on the transit, and the alternatives are noted for the join.

The physics cousins are the extra-dimension dossiers and, for the game's shortcuts, two L3 pages. The Kaluza-Klein hyperspace reading is the negative result: no extra dimension physics knows is a tunnel a ship can fly along; the class bill is B-new. Brane-bulk shortcuts is the one real bulk route, one part in $10^{58}$ today, at a bill of B-new, B-mass and B-caus. For the black holes the cousin is the Kerr interior passage, the analytically extended rotating black hole with a ring you can fly through into another region, and the finding that the real interior has a wall of infinite blueshift where the map runs out; its bill is B-mass and B-caus. For the portals it is the Morris-Thorne traversable wormhole, priced in exotic matter that scales with the throat. The register entry for the class is MAN-1.

The rules it keeps

The Coherence mark is F2, rule-bound: consistent rules with no physical content, applied without exception. Range is a number in light-years and the map enforces it; fuel is a cell per warp and the drive will not fire without one; star colour is gated by drive class and the map refuses a system the ship cannot reach; freighters and fleets have their own ranges. The rules are stable enough that the community computes optimal upgrade layouts, which is a sign that the rules are the drive. The game's economy of antimatter, warp cells and upgrade modules turns the drive into a resource question, and the resource question is honest in its own terms: an unfitted ship cannot cross the galaxy.

None of it is physics. Light-years measure the map but the transit takes seconds regardless of range, warp cells hold antimatter without saying what the antimatter does, and the colour gates are a progression device with an astronomical costume. The mark is F2 and not F1 because the rules are never bent; it is F2 and not F3 because no real limitation is named.

What it ignores

The register's questions. The game never says what a warp is, why range is measured in light-years when the transit's duration is fixed, what antimatter has to do with it or what the tunnel is. The mid-warp interdictions of later updates imply that the tunnel is somewhere ships can meet, which is as close as the game comes to saying it is a place.

Causality is C-silent. The galaxy has a shared clock, players from anywhere meet on any planet in real time, and the transit's independence from distance means a longer warp is not a longer trip in any frame; the game never notices that this is a statement about simultaneity. There is no preferred frame beyond the server's. Last checked 2026-09-12 against the current build: no lore text touches the causal cost of the drive, the black holes or the portals.
