Death's End: curvature propulsion (Liu Cixin, 2010)

Ships that flatten the space behind them and are pulled forward to lightspeed, never past it, leaving trails in which light itself is slowed; the one great space opera that refuses FTL and builds its ending on the refusal.

Kind: fiction · Loophole: L6 · Notability: N2 · Coherence: F4 · Causality: C-owns · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The drive as depicted

Death's End (死神永生, 2010; English translation by Ken Liu, 2016) is the third volume of Remembrance of Earth's Past, and its drive is defined across the Bunker Era and Galaxy Era chapters rather than in one scene. Curvature propulsion works by altering the curvature of space around a ship so that the ship is drawn toward the more curved side, the novel's own analogy being a paper boat on water pushed forward when a drop of soap lowers the surface tension behind it [MED] S3. The engine accelerates the ship to the speed of light; nothing in the book suggests that lightspeed is exceeded, and the whole strategic argument of the middle chapters assumes it is not [HIGH] S1. Thomas Wade's Halo Group develops the technology illegally during the Bunker Era, in defiance of a ban on escapism; Cheng Xin, holding the veto, refuses to let him fight for it, Wade is executed, and the single working engine survives on the ship Halo, which Cheng Xin and AA use to escape the two-dimensional collapse of the solar system [MED] S3 (Wikipedia plot summary, consistent with the text as read).

The trails are the novel's distinctive addition. When the Second Trisolaran Fleet is observed crossing between two interstellar dust clouds in six days where the First Fleet had taken five years, 415 trails are detected, and the conclusion is drawn that ships using curvature propulsion "left behind trails as they accelerated to lightspeed", trails that do not fade but expand and alter the space around them [HIGH] S1. What they alter is the speed of light. The text states it directly: the structure of space itself is changed by the trail, a second curvature-propelled ship placed inside the trail of the first would hardly move, and the speed of light through vacuum is lowered within the trail [HIGH] S1. That fact is the reason the drive is banned: trails would advertise the solar system's position and its capability to any watcher, which under the dark-forest logic of the trilogy invites a strike.

The same effect, pushed to its limit, is the book's ending. The "black domain" plan proposes to use the trails of many curvature engines, spreading outward from the Sun, to lower the speed of light throughout the solar system to 16.7 kilometres per second, below the system's escape velocity, so that the system becomes a reduced-lightspeed black hole from which nothing can leave and into which no attack can propagate: a declaration of harmlessness written into physics [MED] S3. On Planet Gray in the DX3906 system, Guan Yifan shows Cheng Xin "death lines", trails from extreme curvature manipulation in which the speed of light is zero, each one a zero-gravity black hole that, if disturbed, ruptures into a regular trail and can convert a whole system into a black domain [HIGH] S1. Their shuttle flight into the resulting domain costs them almost nineteen million years of Planet Blue's time.

Origin and lineage

Liu Cixin published Death's End in China in 2010 as the conclusion of the trilogy begun with The Three-Body Problem (2008); Ken Liu's English translation appeared from Tor in 2016 and the trilogy has since been adapted for television in China and by Netflix [MED] S3. Liu is an engineer by training and the trilogy's physics is deliberately conservative about the light barrier: the Trisolaran fleet takes four centuries at a tenth of c, the Gravity and Blue Space are sublight ships, and the dark-forest argument depends on the slowness of light for its logic of suspicion.

Curvature propulsion is, mechanically, a warp drive that stops at c. It resembles Alcubierre's 1994 contraction ahead and expansion behind, and the flattened trail is close to a Natário-style statement that what matters is the curvature difference rather than the expansion (Natário 2002); no source has been found in which Liu names Alcubierre or any other real proposal as an influence, and none is asserted here. The reduced-lightspeed trail has a closer real cousin than any warp paper: the Scharnhorst effect, which shows that the speed of light in vacuum is a property of the vacuum's state and can in principle be changed by changing that state. Liu's trails are the Scharnhorst effect with the sign reversed and the magnitude raised from one part in $10^{32}$ to everything. The cosmological variable-speed-of-light proposals of the late 1990s are a further cousin and are not in this catalogue. Nothing in physics descends from the novel; in fiction its black domain is the most original use of a light-speed limit as a plot device since the ansible.

Which loophole, and why

L6, not FTL, with the mechanism borrowed from L2. The brief for this catalogue asks the question directly, L1 or L6, and the text answers it: the ships reach lightspeed and do not exceed it, the trilogy's strategy is built on the fact that nothing outruns light, and the ending is a civilisation choosing to slow light rather than beat it. That is L6 by definition, and the physics cousin for a warp drive that stays below c is the one positive-energy warp result the register still credits: the subluminal constant-velocity shell of Fuchs et al. 2024, a two-Jupiter-mass shell carrying a small shift, which satisfies every energy condition precisely because it never tries to go superluminal (WRP-3). Its bill is B-mass: the shell is planetary in weight and the shift it carries is small. The mechanism Liu describes, curvature ahead pulling and flattened space behind, is the Alcubierre 1994 shape, and the register's bill for that shape at superluminal speed, negative energy larger than the visible universe (ENE-3) and a horizon that puts the front wall outside the crew's control (CON-1), is exactly the bill Liu never has to pay, because his ships stop at c and the register's superluminal pathologies switch off at c (STA-1, HAZ-2).

The L1 reading would say that a ship at c is at the boundary of L1 rather than inside L6, and that the trails, by lowering c, make everything inside them slower rather than faster. That reading has no faster-than-light object anywhere in it, which is the definition of L6. The trails are best read as an L5-adjacent device turned inside out: not a way to send a message faster than light but a way to make sure no message can leave.

The rules it keeps

  • Lightspeed is the ceiling. Stated for the Trisolarans, for humanity and for the Singer's civilisation; the only escape from the two-dimensional collapse is a lightspeed ship, because the collapse itself propagates at lightspeed [HIGH] S1.
  • Dilation at lightspeed is real and paid. The Halo's passengers cross 286 light years in what is a short interval for them and centuries for everyone else; Cheng Xin and Guan Yifan lose nineteen million years to a reduced-lightspeed domain [MED] S3.
  • Trails are permanent, expanding and detectable. Stated when the Trisolaran trails are found, used as the reason for the ban and paid for when the Halo's own trail must be considered [HIGH] S1.
  • Lowered lightspeed lowers everything. Inside a trail a second curvature ship barely moves; inside a black domain computation, communication and thought slow with light; inside a death line every quark is dead [HIGH] S1.
  • The black domain is a one-way door. A civilisation that lowers its lightspeed below escape velocity can never leave and is safe because it can never leave [MED] S3.

This is F4. The novel names a real constraint, the light barrier, keeps it and builds its central moral choice (escape versus safety, Wade versus Cheng Xin) and its ending on the cost of keeping it. Its second invention, the trail, is a physical cost of the drive that the plot pays in full.

What it ignores

The novel forbids the thing that would bite, which is the C-owns escape in GRADING.md: no ship or signal ever outruns light, so no observer ever sees an effect before its cause. Sophons, the trilogy's faster-than-light communicators, are the exception and they belong to The Three-Body Problem rather than to this drive; they are an L5 device and would be scored on their own page. Within Death's End the drive itself never raises a causal question because it never can. The novel is silent on the physics of the trail: how curvature manipulation changes the vacuum's light speed, why the change is permanent, why it spreads. These are absences in the mechanism, not in the causality, and they are dated here to the 2016 translation.
