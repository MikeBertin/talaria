Stargate: the ships' hyperdrives

The starships of SG-1 and Atlantis open a window into hyperspace and cross galaxies at speeds set by whose engine is fitted; a tiered technology, distinct from the gates, that the franchise rations by power source.

Kind: fiction · Loophole: L4 · Notability: N3 · Coherence: F2 · Causality: C-silent · Last reviewed: 2026-09-12

The drive as depicted

The Stargate franchise has two ways between the stars, and this dossier is about the second. The gates, stable wormholes between fixed rings, have their own dossier in the L3 catalogue. Ships have hyperdrives, and the hyperdrive is shown long before it is explained. A Goa'uld ha'tak mothership carries SG-1 from Chulak toward Earth in the first-season finale "Within the Serpent's Grasp" (1998), the ship entering and leaving hyperspace through a bright window that opens ahead of it [MED] S3. From then on every spacefaring power in the series has a hyperdrive, and the drama is in whose is faster.

The rules are stated by incident rather than by lecture. In "Enemies" (season 5, episode 1, 29 June 2001) SG-1's captured ha'tak is thrown into another galaxy by the shock of a supernova its crew had set off; the franchise wiki places it four million light-years out with a return time of a hundred and twenty-five years at top speed, and the episode's resolution is that Replicators aboard modify the engines so the ship exceeds its fastest velocity by eight hundred times, making the trip home short [HIGH] S3 (the episode's own plot; the distance and time figures are the wiki's [MED] S3). Earth's first hyperdrive ship, the X-303 Prometheus, is introduced in "Prometheus" (season 6, episode 11, 23 August 2002) with a naquadria-powered hyperdrive that Earth's engineers cannot make work until a Goa'uld's knowledge corrects the error; hijacked, the ship jumps and emerges twelve hundred light-years from Earth with no reference points from which Carter can plot a course home [HIGH] S3. Naquadria, the unstable isotope, is the recurring limit on Earth's early drives: in "Memento" (season 7) the drive fails and the ship is stranded until the engine can be rebuilt [MED] S3. The solution the franchise settles on is borrowed engines. The Daedalus-class ships of Stargate Atlantis carry Asgard hyperdrives and can cross the three million light-years from Earth to the Pegasus galaxy in eighteen days on their own power, or in about four days when a Zero Point Module is fitted [MED] S3. The Ancients' ships and the Ori's are faster still; the Wraith's hive ships are slower and must drop out of hyperspace between legs of a long journey [MED] S3.

The hyperspace itself is a bright, streaked corridor seen from the bridge, entered through a "hyperspace window" that the ship opens ahead of it and left through the same [MED] S3. It is never given a physics on screen beyond the name and the observation that ships in it cannot be seen or fired on from normal space; time aboard runs as it does on Earth, and nobody returns from a hyperspace trip having aged differently [MED] S3. Communication uses "subspace", a separate word the franchise applies to signals rather than ships [MED] S3.

Origin and lineage

Stargate SG-1 ran ten seasons from 1997 to 2007 under Brad Wright and Jonathan Glassner, and Stargate Atlantis five from 2004 to 2009 under Wright and Robert C. Cooper, both from the premise of the 1994 film by Roland Emmerich and Dean Devlin; the film has no starships and the hyperdrive is entirely the series' invention [HIGH] S3. The word "hyperdrive" and the window-into-a-corridor visual are the common property of television science fiction after Star Wars (1977), and the series never claims otherwise. No real proposal is named for the ships' drives, and this dossier asserts no source; the gates, by contrast, are called wormholes on screen and have a physics lineage that the gate dossier carries.

Which loophole, and why

L4, leave the manifold. A ship opens a window, enters a region called hyperspace with its own rules (invisibility from normal space, distances covered in days that light covers in millions of years) and re-emerges. It is not fast in our space (L1), it does not carry a bubble (L2) and it does not use a tunnel between fixed ends, which is what the gates do (L3). The franchise's own vocabulary keeps the two apart: ships "enter hyperspace", gates "establish a wormhole".

The physics cousins are the extra-dimension dossiers. The Kaluza-Klein hyperspace reading is the negative finding for the whole class: extra dimensions in physics are compact or warped, share our light cone and are not a corridor; its bill is B-new and the conclusion that hyperspace as television uses the word has no counterpart. Brane-bulk shortcuts is the one real bulk shortcut, one part in $10^{58}$ today, positive-energy by construction, with a bill of B-new, B-mass and B-caus; on the brane it looks like FTL with every consequence of CAU-1. The register entry for the class is MAN-1. For the gates the cousin is the Morris-Thorne traversable wormhole, whose bill, exotic matter scaling with throat size, is priced in the gate dossier and not here.

The rules it keeps

The Coherence mark is F2, rule-bound: the drive has internally consistent rules with no physical content, and the series applies them well. The rules are a ladder of speeds by builder (Goa'uld below Asgard below Ancient and Ori, with the Wraith slower and Earth climbing the ladder by borrowing), a dependence on power source (naquadah, naquadria, a ZPM) that sets both range and reliability, and a distinction between interstellar drives and the intergalactic ones that only the top of the ladder has. Earth's progress through the ladder is one of the series' long arcs and is never skipped: Prometheus is unreliable, Daedalus is Asgard-built, and later ships get the Asgard core outright. The rule that ships in hyperspace cannot be engaged from outside is used tactically and never broken. Jump durations are consistent to within the needs of an episode.

None of it is physics. Hyperspace has no stated metric, the "window" has no stated cost, and no constraint from the register is named anywhere. That is F2 exactly as GRADING.md defines it: hyperspace lanes and jump cooldowns.

What it ignores

Every register question. The series never says what hyperspace is, what opening a window costs in energy beyond "a lot", why speed depends on builder rather than on physics, or what a ship's arrival before its own light does to the order of events for anyone watching. The time-travel stories the franchise does tell ("1969", "2010", "Moebius") use the gate and solar flares, never the hyperdrive [MED] S3, so the drive is never made to face its own implication.

Causality is C-silent. Ships cross galaxies in days and communicate by subspace in real time, so the setting has an unexamined universal now, and no episode asks what it is. There is no stated rule that would prevent the antitelephone, no preferred frame, and no story in which the hyperdrive produces a paradox. Last checked 2026-09-12 across both series: the drive's causal cost is never raised.
