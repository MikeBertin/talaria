# Starfield (2023): the grav drive

*A ship component with a jump range in light years, a fuel gauge in helium-3 and a backstory in which testing it stripped the Earth of its magnetosphere.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L3 · **Notability:** N3 · **Coherence:** F2 · **Causality:** C-silent · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

*Starfield* (Bethesda Game Studios, directed by Todd Howard, released September 2023) is set in 2330 in the Settled Systems, a region of roughly a hundred star systems within about fifty light years of Sol. Every ship the player flies has a grav drive, and every trip between systems is a "grav jump": the player picks a destination on the star map, the drive spools for a few seconds while the ship is vulnerable, a distortion spreads from the hull and the ship is gone, arriving at the destination system with no transit shown **[HIGH] S1**. The drive is a swappable module with numbers. Its "grav jump thrust" sets the maximum jump in light years, reduced by the mass of the ship it is bolted to; no drive in the game exceeds thirty light years in a single jump, so longer trips are chains of jumps through intermediate systems; and each jump burns helium-3 fuel in proportion to distance **[HIGH] S1**. The drive also supplies the ship's artificial gravity, so a ship with its grav drive powered down floats **[HIGH] S1**. The in-game name is the Graviton Loop Array; a longer name, Graviton Loop Field Array, was given by Howard in interview **[MED] S3**.

Nothing in the game states what the drive does to space. The visual is a bending of the starfield around the ship and a flash; the word is "grav"; that is all. The lore does something else. In the main quest "Unearthed" the player enters the ruined NASA launch tower on Earth and reads Dr Victor Aiza's terminals: an artefact recovered from Mars, a prototype drive connected to it and a supercomputer, and notes recording that testing of early drives on Luna was destabilising Earth's magnetosphere; a final note shows Aiza knew and pressed on **[HIGH] S1**. The wider timeline the game gives is that the drive was invented in the 2130s, that the magnetosphere collapsed in 2203 and Earth was abandoned, and that the perfected drive is what let humanity leave **[MED] S3** (in-game dates as compiled by the wikis; the Aiza notes are read directly). The game's two Starborn guides then argue, in dialogue, whether the drive was worth the Earth **[HIGH] S1**. That is the one cost of faster-than-light travel the game prices, and it is priced as backstory rather than as a running constraint.

The Starborn, and the player once they become one, jump between universes through the Unity; that is a different mechanism with a different cost and outside this dossier, except to note that the artefacts that power it are also the origin of the drive **[HIGH] S1**.

## Origin and lineage

Bethesda's first new setting in decades, announced in 2018 and released in 2023, with Howard as director and executive producer **[HIGH] S3**. The studio described its aim as "NASA-punk", a near-future look with visible engineering, and the grav drive fits that: it is a part with a stat block, not a mystery. No real proposal is named in the game. "Graviton" is the only physics word attached, and it is used as it is in *Event Horizon*, as a noun that sounds like an explanation. The catalogue carries that film separately ([Event Horizon](event-horizon-gravity-drive.md)), and it is on record as an influence on the game's tone **[MED] S3**; whether the drive's name follows the film's is not sourced and not claimed. The lore device of a drive that ruins its home world is the game's own.

No forward influence on physics is claimed.

## Which loophole, and why

**L3, take a shortcut.** A grav jump is a point-to-point displacement with no transit, no lane and no other space named; the ship is in one system, spools, and is in another. There is no fast motion through ordinary space (in-system flight is slow and the game's "boost" is chemical) and no separate hyperspace with rules of its own. The jump is a fold with a range limit, which puts it in the L3 group beside the Macross fold and the Battlestar jump rather than with warp drives or hyperspace lanes.

The cousin is [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), the traversable wormhole made on demand, with the [Kerr interior passage](../physics/kerr-interior-passage.md) as the gravitational-collapse alternative the word "grav" gestures at. The Morris–Thorne bill is negative energy at the throat, a far mouth delivered in advance at ordinary speed and a time machine on any use that beats light; the Kerr bill is a black hole of tens of thousands of solar masses and a route through built-in closed timelike curves. The grav drive pays the delivery bill without the game naming it: the Settled Systems are reached by a chain of jumps because no single jump exceeds thirty light years, which is infrastructure by another name. It pays nothing else. A jump opened from the departure end to bare coordinates is the object [CON-2](../../constraints/con-2.md) says cannot be aimed, and the game aims it every time.

## The rules it keeps

- Range is a number, set by drive power and reduced by ship mass, and no single jump exceeds thirty light years **[HIGH] S1**.
- Fuel is helium-3, consumed per jump in proportion to distance, and a ship without fuel cannot jump **[HIGH] S1**.
- The drive spools before it fires and the ship can be attacked meanwhile; more power shortens the spool **[HIGH] S1**.
- The drive is also the ship's gravity, and loses that function when unpowered **[HIGH] S1**.
- The technology had a cost: early testing destroyed Earth's magnetosphere, stated in Aiza's notes and argued over by the Starborn **[HIGH] S1**.

These rules are consistent and enforced by the game's systems; the range and fuel rules shape every trip. They have no physical content: nothing says what a jump does, why range scales with mass or why a drive that folds space also makes gravity. The magnetosphere backstory is a cost stated once and then done with. F2 by the grading scheme, rule-bound without physics. The drive would reach F3 if the Earth backstory were a limitation applied to current travel; it is a history lesson, and current jumps cost fuel only.

## What it ignores

Time. A jump of thirty light years is instantaneous by the player's clock and by the destination's, since the game's universal calendar advances only with in-system travel and rest, and no character in any faction ever asks whether a jump can arrive before it left or what a jump means for the order of events between systems **[HIGH] S1**. There is no preferred frame, no simultaneity rule and no consequence drawn. C-silent. The game's multiverse mechanic is not an exception: the Unity moves the player between universes, not between times.

The register's other questions, what a grav jump does to space, what holds anything open, what the energy budget of a thirty-light-year fold is and how a jump to bare coordinates is aimed, are unasked. The only engagement is the magnetosphere, which is an engineering-side-effect story with no physical mechanism offered.
