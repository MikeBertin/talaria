# Vinge's Zones of Thought: FTL only where physics permits it (1992)

*The one setting in which the laws governing faster-than-light travel change with where you are: no FTL near the galactic core, an ultradrive out toward the rim, and the boundary as a wall you can be stranded behind.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L4 · **Notability:** N2 · **Coherence:** F4 · **Causality:** C-dodges · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

*A Fire Upon the Deep* (Tor, 1992) defines its physics in its opening chapters and its map. The galaxy is divided into concentric Zones of Thought. In the Unthinking Depths around the core nothing much can think; in the Slow Zone, where Earth is, biological intelligence works, true artificial intelligence does not and nothing travels faster than light; in the Beyond, from the Slow Zone's edge outward, FTL travel, FTL communication and artificial minds all work, better the higher one goes; and in the Transcend beyond that live the Powers, minds that have passed beyond anything the lower zones can follow **[HIGH] S3** (Wikipedia, "A Fire Upon the Deep", setting; the novel from memory). The zones are not sharp lines and not fixed: they have layers (Bottom, Middle and Top of the Beyond) and they shift, and the book's climax is a deliberate shift, when the weapon called Countermeasure pushes the Slow Zone outward by thousands of light years and strands the Blight's fleet, and the heroes, inside the Slowness **[HIGH] S1** (from memory of the novel's ending; stable).

The drive is the ultradrive. It moves a ship by many small jumps, and its efficiency rises with distance from the core: at the Bottom of the Beyond jumps are short and slow and the drive fails altogether at the boundary with the Slow Zone, so ships that work near the Bottom carry a Bussard ramjet as a fallback; in the Middle and High Beyond the same drive makes light years an hour **[MED] S3** (Wikipedia; the ramjet fallback and the depth dependence as stated there, the rate from memory). The ship Out of Band II's chase across the Beyond is the novel's demonstration of all of this, with the Zone boundary as a hazard the navigator watches like weather **[MED] S1**. Communication across the Beyond is by ultrawave, at speeds that depend on depth in the same way, carried by a network the novel calls the Net and renders as Usenet-style message headers **[HIGH] S3** (Wikipedia). The Slow Zone's physics is ours: the sequel *A Deepness in the Sky* (1999) is set there, its ships are ramscoops crawling between stars over decades, its crews sleep in coldsleep and its history is a history of relativistic time debt, with no FTL of any kind **[HIGH] S1** (from memory of the novel; well known).

Vinge stated the purpose of the construction. The Zones were "my attempt to get around the limitations" that the technological Singularity imposes on science fiction writers; the Singularity "is spread out sideways"; and the intermediate zone, in Vinge's words, is one where "superhuman intelligence was not possible but faster-than-light drives were", so that one universe could carry three subgenres of hard science fiction, "one is where faster-than-light travel is not possible" **[HIGH] S3** (Lightspeed Magazine interview, May 2012, by John Joseph Adams and David Barr Kirtley, read in full).

## Origin and lineage

Vernor Vinge, a mathematician and computer scientist at San Diego State, first used the Zones in the novella "The Blabber" (1988), set after the events of *Fire* **[HIGH] S3** (Wikipedia). The 1992 novel won the Hugo Award, and the setting produced *A Deepness in the Sky* (1999) and *The Children of the Sky* (2011). The physics is not borrowed from any proposal: the zones exist because Vinge wanted to keep writing about FTL and about the Singularity while believing, on the evidence, that the first is forbidden and the second is coming, and his solution was to make the forbidding regional **[HIGH] S3** (Lightspeed 2012). What the construction amounts to in the register's terms, a region-dependent maximal speed fixed to the galaxy, is discussed under causality below; Vinge does not describe it in those terms and no physics paper cites the novel.

Lineage: [Le Guin's ansible goes out into the field](../../lineage/le-guin-ansible-outward.md).

## Which loophole, and why

L4 by the catalogue's placement and by the drive's presentation: a jump drive that moves a ship in a series of leaps is the pulp hyperspace jump ([Asimov's Jump](foundation-hyperspace-jumps.md) is the template), and the novel says nothing about what is between the leaps. But the interesting move is not the drive, it is the map. Vinge's real loophole is that the laws of physics are a function of position, and FTL of any mechanism is allowed only where that function permits. That is a different kind of claim from any other work in the catalogue, and it has a physics cousin of its own.

The cousins are three. [Lorentz-violating frameworks](../physics/lorentz-violating-frameworks.md), the Standard-Model Extension and Hořava-Lifshitz gravity, are the physics of a world with a preferred frame in which a species may have its own maximal speed without an antitelephone; their bill is a new set of fields (B-new), a frame that special relativity does not supply (B-caus) and, so far, every measured coefficient consistent with zero. Vinge's Zones are that framework with the coefficients made a function of galactic radius. [The relativistic rocket](../physics/relativistic-rocket.md) is the Slow Zone's sublight alternative, and *A Deepness in the Sky* is one of the few space operas that pays its mass-ratio bill on the page. And [Kaluza-Klein and the hyperspace reading](../physics/kaluza-klein-hyperspace.md) is the register's [MAN-1](../../constraints/man-1.md) for the ultradrive itself: whatever the jumps are, extra dimensions in physics do not provide them.

## The rules it keeps

- **No FTL in the Slow Zone**, stated as a law of nature and held absolutely: the ramscoop fallback near the boundary, the Blight's fleet stranded by the shift, and an entire sequel set under the rule **[HIGH] S1**.
- **Drive and communication efficiency rise with distance from the core**, with named layers of the Beyond and a boundary that is a navigational hazard **[MED] S3**.
- **The Zones move**, slowly on their own and violently under Countermeasure, and a shift is permanent for anyone caught on the wrong side **[HIGH] S1**.
- **Intelligence is capped by the same map**: no true AI in the Slow Zone, and in the Depths not even human thought **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Communication is delayed by depth** and bandwidth is scarce, which the Net's headers make visible **[HIGH] S3**.

The mark is F4. The work names a real constraint (nothing travels faster than light, and in our part of the galaxy that is simply true), keeps it where it applies, and builds its whole plot around the cost: the chase is a race to reach a place where physics allows escape before the physics changes, and the ending is the constraint winning. GRADING.md lists Vinge as the F4 example, and the sequel is the cost paid in full.

## What it ignores

The novel never mentions causality, the antitelephone or what an FTL trip looks like from a moving frame, and neither does the interview, as of 2026-09-12. The mark is nevertheless C-dodges, because the stated rule of the fiction supplies the register's escape whether or not the author meant it to. [CAU-4](../../constraints/cau-4.md) says FTL avoids paradox if its speed is fixed in one frame by a property of the medium rather than of the laws; Vinge's ultradrive speed is fixed by position in a structure at rest with respect to the galaxy, which is exactly such a frame, and the Zones are a property of the medium in the plainest sense the novel can give. The rule answers the constraint without naming it, and is scored as a rule, not an engagement. The alternative reading, C-silent on the ground that the frame is inferred rather than stated, is noted; this dossier takes the rule at its word. What the ultradrive spends, what the jumps are and why the Slowness has an edge at all are never addressed, and the last is the point: the novel presents the map as a given, like the speed of light.
