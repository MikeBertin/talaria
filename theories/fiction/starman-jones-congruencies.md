# Starman Jones: the congruencies (Heinlein 1953)

*Space folded back on itself at charted points that must be hit exactly, with the tables done by hand; get the arithmetic wrong and the ship is nowhere.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L3 · **Notability:** N3 · **Coherence:** F3 · **Causality:** C-silent · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

Robert A. Heinlein's *Starman Jones* (Charles Scribner's Sons, 1953) is a novel about the Astrogators' Guild, and its drive is whatever the astrogators compute **[HIGH] S3**. The starship *Asgard* travels between stars by passing through anomalies, points in space where, as the text has it, space is folded back on itself so that a long distance becomes no distance; a ship that reaches the anomaly on exactly the right course and speed makes a transition and is instantly elsewhere, at the congruent point near the destination **[MED] S3**. The novel gives the geometry a rule: "Anomalies are always flat, otherwise they couldn't fit together", which is to say the two ends must be congruent surfaces for the fold to join them **[MED] S3** (quoted on the MathFiction database from the text; not verified against a copy for this dossier). Reference summaries call the anomalies Horst congruencies and the drive the Horst-Conrad; this catalogue has not confirmed the names against the text **[LOW] S3**. Space is curved near stars and planets and nearly flat between them, so the anomalies lie in flat regions, and a ship must reach them by ordinary flight and hit them at the computed speed **[MED] S3**.

The whole story is in the computing. The ship's computer is a calculator with no readable output; astrogators enter figures and read results through printed tables, converting decimal to binary and back, and an astrogator's competence is the speed and accuracy with which he does this under time pressure as the ship approaches an anomaly **[HIGH] S3**. Max Jones, a farm boy with a photographic memory who has read his uncle's astrogation manuals, catches a computational error made by the elderly captain during an approach; nobody believes him; the transition is made with the wrong figures and the *Asgard* comes out in uncharted space, lost, with no anomaly in its charts **[HIGH] S3**. The assistant astrogator destroys the ship's tables before dying, and Max, who has them by heart, reconstructs them and computes a reverse transition that brings the ship back **[HIGH] S3**. Nothing about the drive itself is described: no field, no energy source, no sensation of transit. The physics is that a fold exists at a charted place, that it is entered by exactness and that error is unrecoverable without the numbers.

## Origin and lineage

Heinlein's juveniles of the early 1950s were written to a standard of technical plausibility, and *Starman Jones* keeps the drive's mechanism out of view while making its operation the plot **[MED] S3**. The instant transition between fixed points, the charts, the exact approach and the ship lost by a navigational slip are the pattern later followed by the tramlines of the [Alderson drive](alderson-drive.md) and the calculated jumps of [Battlestar Galactica](battlestar-galactica-ftl-jump.md), and the "folded back on itself" image is the same picture the 1984 *Dune* film calls folding space ([Dune](dune-holtzman-fold-space.md)). No real proposal is named; the novel predates the traversable wormhole by thirty-five years and the word "wormhole" by four. The catalogue cousin is [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), and the flat-surface rule has a striking match in [Visser's thin-shell wormholes](../physics/visser-thin-shell-1989.md), in which two flat regions are cut and identified and the transition is across a surface with no interior: Heinlein's "always flat, otherwise they couldn't fit together" is Visser's construction stated as navigation. That is a coincidence of picture, not an influence, and is recorded as such. One reviewer notes that the congruencies could be read as wormholes; the novel does not say so **[LOW] S3**.

## Which loophole, and why

L3, a shortcut with fixed endpoints. The ship is at an anomaly and then at its congruent partner, with the intervening distance turned to nothing; no other space is entered and nothing outruns light in the space it is in. The cousin is [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), whose bill is negative energy at the throat scaling with its size, mass beyond engineering, a pre-laid route and a time machine once a mouth is moved. Heinlein pays the third by making the anomalies natural and charted, gives the join no matter at all and never touches the first, second or fourth.

## The rules it keeps

- **Anomalies are where they are.** The ship flies to them at sublight speed and cannot transition elsewhere; the loss of the *Asgard* is the loss of the chart **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Exact approach.** Course and speed must match the computed figures at the moment of transition; a slip sends the ship to an unknown place **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Congruence.** The two ends of a fold must fit, and the text gives flatness as the reason **[MED] S3**.
- **Human arithmetic.** The tables are the bottleneck and the plot; there is no autopilot and the rule is never broken for convenience **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Reversibility.** A transition can be undone by recomputing it, which is how the ship gets home **[HIGH] S3**.

F3: a stated limitation, the charted anomaly with its exact approach, applied without exception and given a geometric reason (flatness, congruence). Not F2, because the flatness rule is a claim about the shape of the shortcut rather than an arbitrary cooldown; not F4, because no real constraint is named and the cost the story is built on is arithmetic, not physics.

## What it ignores

A transition is instantaneous between stars, and the novel never says in what frame, never states a preferred frame and never asks what a transition and its reverse do to the order of events. No time loop appears and none is forbidden. Absence dated: as of 2026-09-12 the novel is silent on the causality of its transitions. C-silent. The energy, stability and tidal entries are untouched because the drive has no described mechanism; the only cost is the calculation, and the only hazard is being lost.
