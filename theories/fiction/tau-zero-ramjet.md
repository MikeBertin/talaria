# Tau Zero's ramjet

*Anderson's Bussard ramjet loses its brakes, and the crew ride time dilation past the end of the universe rather than around the light barrier.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L6 · **Notability:** N2 · **Coherence:** F4 · **Causality:** C-owns · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

The *Leonora Christine* is an interstellar ramjet carrying twenty-five men and twenty-five women from Earth to Beta Virginis, about thirty light years away, on a flight planned to take five years by ship time and thirty-three by Earth's **[HIGH] S3**. The ship scoops interstellar hydrogen, fuses it and throws it back; the same fields that gather fuel shield the crew from the hydrogen they are hitting **[HIGH] S3**. The novel names its physics in its title. Tau is the time-contraction factor, the reciprocal of the Lorentz factor, and Anderson defines it in the narration as the square root of one minus the square of the ship's speed over the speed of light; the closer the speed comes to light's, the closer tau comes to zero **[HIGH] S3** (formula as given by Wikipedia and by a physics-journal treatment; the chapter was not identified). The mission's planned peak is tau of about 0.015 **[MED] S3**. The prose is narrative "interspersed with paragraphs" in which Anderson explains relativity and the ship's mechanics, and a chapter-13 image of "two globes, fiery blue ahead, deep crimson aft" is the Doppler shift of the sky seen from the bridge **[MED] S3**.

The accident defines the book. Before the halfway point the ship passes through a small nebula that damages the deceleration field generators **[HIGH] S3**. The drive cannot be shut down for repairs, because the ram fields are also the radiation shielding and the engines' own radiation makes the damaged section unapproachable, so the ship can neither stop accelerating nor be fixed **[HIGH] S3**. The only course is to accelerate harder, out of the galaxy and its cluster, into intergalactic space thin enough to switch off and repair **[MED] S3**. Tau falls without limit. Ship-years become galactic ages; the crew watch the universe age, contract into a monobloc and re-expand, and finally decelerate into a new cosmos and land on a planet with bluish-green vegetation **[HIGH] S3**. Paul Gilster quotes the novel's own image of the ship as "the barest glint of metal in that vast web of forces" **[MED] S3**.

## Origin and lineage

Poul Anderson published "To Outlive Eternity" in *Galaxy* in two parts, June and August 1967, and expanded it as *Tau Zero* (Doubleday, 1970); the novel was a 1971 Hugo nominee **[HIGH] S3**. The physics parent is real and on the register's control-group dossier: Robert Bussard's "Galactic Matter and Interstellar Flight", Astronautica Acta 6, 179 (1960), the interstellar ramjet, which Wikipedia says the novel popularised **[HIGH] S3**. Whether Anderson credits Bussard in the text or in any foreword was not established; one reviewer says the term "Bussard ramjet" never appears in the novel **[LOW] S5**. The physicist Al Jackson, who hosted Anderson at a 1979 AIAA dinner, has written that Anderson and Bussard had never met and that his guess is Anderson took the idea from Carl Sagan's exposition in *Intelligent Life in the Universe* **[MED] S3**. That is the strongest lineage claim available: Bussard 1960, through Sagan, to Anderson 1967, with the middle link a physicist's informed guess rather than Anderson's own statement.

The novel fed physics teaching rather than physics proposals. Blatter and Greber, "Tau Zero: In the cockpit of a Bussard ramjet", American Journal of Physics 85, 915 (2017), take the novel's scenario as the worked problem, treating an ideal ramjet that retains no matter as capable of endless acceleration and comparing the result with Anderson's numbers **[HIGH] S2** (record verified; full text not accessible). The publisher's line attributing "the ultimate hard science fiction novel" to James Blish is on every edition, but the original review was not found **[LOW] S4**.

## Which loophole, and why

L6, not FTL. This is the [relativistic rocket](../physics/relativistic-rocket.md) of the physics catalogue, in its ramjet variant, with the kinematics done exactly and the engineering assumed. The cousin's bill is B-mass, energy beyond engineering; the ramjet trades the fuel for a scoop the size of a small planet and an unsolved plasma problem, and the modern verdict on Bussard's design, that drag exceeds thrust and bremsstrahlung losses swamp the fusion yield by a factor of order a billion, is in the cousin dossier **[HIGH] S1** (via the register's sources). Anderson's ship pays none of that bill on the page and all of the kinematic one: the novel's whole argument is that at constant acceleration proper time grows only logarithmically with distance, so a crew can outlive a universe without ever reaching c.

## The rules it keeps

- **Nothing exceeds light.** Tau approaches zero and never reaches it; the drama is the approach **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Time dilation is charged in full and in one direction.** The crew accept early that Earth is gone; a review cites p. 83 for the recognition that millions of years have passed and the human race is most likely extinct **[MED] S5**. There is no return and the book never pretends one.
- **The ship obeys its own engineering.** The fields that are propulsion are also shielding, which is why the accident is a trap and not an inconvenience; the decision to seek intergalactic vacuum follows from the density the scoop needs **[HIGH] S3**.
- **The cosmology is the cosmology of 1970.** A closed, oscillating universe with a monobloc between cycles, then a leading theory and now excluded by dark energy; reviewers note that the ship's orbit around the monobloc treats it as a point in pre-existing space where the physics has spacetime itself contracting **[MED] S5**.

F4: the work names a real constraint, builds every turn of its plot on the cost of that constraint, and gets the kinematic law right. The datedness of its cosmology is a finding about 1970, not about the grade.

## What it ignores

Nothing that bears on causality. The mark is C-owns because the novel forbids FTL absolutely and makes the impossibility of return its subject. What it does not treat is the signal side: contact with Earth is simply lost, and no fetched source shows the text asking what a message home would cost or how long it would take, so LOR-3 is scored silent. It also ignores, as the physics cousin notes, the engineering objections to the ramjet that were published after it (Fishback 1969 on scoop stresses, Heppenheimer 1978 on radiative losses), which is a matter of date, and the radiation environment of a crew at extreme Lorentz factor, which is not mentioned in any source fetched **[LOW] S3**.
