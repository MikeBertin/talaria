# Interstellar: the wormhole near Saturn (2014)

*A wormhole nobody on Earth built, placed by "they", with the clocks at Gargantua done to the equations and a physicist's book to show the working.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L3 · **Notability:** N1 · **Coherence:** F4 · **Causality:** C-owns · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

*Interstellar* (directed by Christopher Nolan, released 5 November 2014 in the United States) has one faster-than-light device and it is not on the ship **[HIGH] S3**. A wormhole appeared near Saturn forty-eight years before the film's present, and NASA, working in secret, has been sending probes and then people through it to a planetary system in another galaxy around the black hole Gargantua **[HIGH] S3**. The Endurance takes two years to reach Saturn at ordinary speeds; the wormhole is the whole of the shortcut, and the film is explicit that it did not occur naturally: it was placed by beings the characters call "they", whom the film's ending identifies as humans from the far future acting through higher dimensions **[HIGH] S3**. Romilly explains the geometry to Cooper on the way out with a sheet of paper folded so that a pen pierces two distant points at once, and the exchange ends with the wormhole not being a hole but a sphere, which is what the crew then flies into **[MED] S3**.

The film's second physics set piece is not the wormhole but the clock at Gargantua. Miller's planet orbits close enough to the black hole that, as Romilly puts it, "Every hour we spend down there will be maybe... seven years back on Earth", and a landing that goes wrong costs the crew twenty-three years of the Endurance's time while Romilly waits in orbit **[HIGH] S1** (line verified via Wikiquote, S3 transcription). The film's climax sends Cooper into Gargantua and into a tesseract, a construction in which time is laid out as a spatial dimension, from which he sends data to his daughter in the past by pushing gravity through the books and the watch in her bedroom **[HIGH] S3**. "They" turn out to be "us": the beings who placed the wormhole and built the tesseract are the humans the mission saves **[HIGH] S3**.

What the film does not show is anything holding the wormhole open. No exotic matter is named, no throat radius is given and the transit is a short flight through a tunnel of distorted starlight. The visual is not decoration: the wormhole and the black hole were rendered from ray-tracing code built on equations supplied by Kip Thorne, and the code produced two peer-reviewed papers **[HIGH] S1**.

## Origin and lineage

Thorne was executive producer and science consultant; the project began as a treatment by Thorne and Lynda Obst, and Thorne's rule for the production was that nothing on screen should violate established physics and that every speculation should be at least defensible **[MED] S2** (his own account in *The Science of Interstellar*, Norton, 2014). The wormhole is a direct descendant of [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), the paper that Sagan's *Contact* request produced, and Thorne is the link between the two films; see [Contact](contact-sagan-machine.md). The companion book has a part titled "The Wormhole" with chapters "Wormholes", "Visualizing Interstellar's wormhole" and "Discovering the wormhole: gravitational waves", a chapter "Miller's planet", a part "Extreme Physics" with chapters "The fourth and fifth dimensions", "Bulk beings" and "Gravitational anomalies", and a climax part with "The tesseract" and "Messaging the past" **[HIGH] S3** (table of contents verified from library records). In that book Thorne says the wormhole's existence requires exotic matter with negative energy that no known physics provides, and attributes its construction and maintenance to the bulk beings, which is the same move Sagan made in 1985 with a lost civilisation **[MED] S2**.

The lineage runs outward as well. James, von Tunzelmann, Franklin and Thorne, "Visualizing Interstellar's Wormhole", Am. J. Phys. 83, 486 (2015), arXiv:1502.03809, describes the "three-parameter wormhole" used to scope the film's geometry, the ray-tracing from a camera through it to celestial spheres and the motion of star images and the Einstein ring as the camera moves **[HIGH] S1**. A companion paper on gravitational lensing by Gargantua appeared in Classical and Quantum Gravity the same year **[HIGH] S3**. Miller's planet is Thorne's response to a director's demand: Nolan wanted one hour to equal seven years, Thorne first said it was impossible, then found that a planet skimming a black hole spinning within a hair of the maximum allowed rate could do it **[MED] S3** (Thorne's account, retold in reviews of the book). The bulk, the fifth dimension in which "they" live and from which the wormhole is placed, is the language of the brane-world models catalogued at [brane-bulk shortcuts](../physics/brane-bulk-shortcuts.md) and [Kaluza–Klein hyperspace](../physics/kaluza-klein-hyperspace.md), and Thorne's book says so.

## Which loophole, and why

L3, a shortcut. The Endurance never exceeds light speed; it takes two years to reach Saturn and enters a tunnel whose far mouth is in another galaxy. The film adds a second move as explanation rather than mechanism: the wormhole was placed from the bulk, a higher-dimensional space, so the story's account of who built it is L4-flavoured while the ship's journey is a plain L3 traversal. The cousin is [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), whose bill is negative energy at the throat scaling with its radius, mass beyond any engineering, a route laid in advance and a time machine on assembly. The film pays the third by making the wormhole a gift and, in its tesseract sequence, spends the fourth on purpose. The tunnel's appearance is the [Visser thin-shell](../physics/visser-thin-shell-1989.md) intuition made visible: a spherical mouth in flat space, with the distortion in a shell around it.

## The rules it keeps

- **Only the wormhole is faster than light.** Everything else is a rocket: two years to Saturn, slingshots around Gargantua to change orbit, fuel budgets that force the choice between Mann's planet and Edmunds' **[HIGH] S3**.
- **The wormhole was placed, and by someone who could pay for it.** The film says outright that wormholes do not occur naturally and that this one was put near Saturn by "they"; the ending identifies "they" as future humans working from the bulk **[HIGH] S3**. This is CON-2 and STA-2 answered by fiat, and the fiat is named on screen.
- **Clocks near Gargantua run slow, by the equations.** The seven-years-per-hour figure was computed by Thorne for a near-extremal spinning black hole and the film spends twenty-three years of Romilly's life on it **[MED] S2**. Time dilation is the engine of the third act, not a footnote.
- **Gravity crosses the bulk; nothing else does.** The film's rule for how "they" and Cooper act on the past is that gravity alone leaks between dimensions, which is why the message is sent by moving books and a watch hand **[HIGH] S3**. The rule is stated in Thorne's book as the brane-world statement that gravity propagates in the bulk while matter and light are confined to the brane **[MED] S2**.
- **The wormhole has a visual physics.** The lensing, the Einstein ring and the star-image motion are computed from a stated metric and published **[HIGH] S1**.

F4 on the GRADING.md definition, and GRADING.md names *Interstellar* as an F4 example: the film names real constraints (nothing outruns light in flat space, a wormhole is not natural, clocks near a spinning black hole run slow) and builds its plot on their cost, the twenty-three lost years above all.

## What it ignores

The wormhole itself is the one place where the film stops short of its own standard. No exotic matter is mentioned on screen; the companion book supplies it, along with the admission that the wormhole is the least defensible object in the film **[MED] S2**. The quantum inequalities, the tidal bound at the throat and topological censorship are all in the book's territory and none is on screen. On causality the film does not dodge and does not stay silent: the tesseract sequence sends information into the past, and the plot depends on it arriving, so the film admits that its physics permits messages to earlier times and uses that as its resolution, with the loop closed (Cooper is the "ghost" Murph saw as a child) rather than paradoxical **[HIGH] S3**. Thorne's chapter title is "Messaging the past" **[HIGH] S3**. That is C-owns: the work admits the implication and builds on it. What it never does is connect the wormhole to the same problem; the shortcut near Saturn is treated as a door, and the time loop is a separate mechanism inside the black hole. Dated absence: as of 2026-09-12 no cut of the film or edition of the book treats the Saturn wormhole as the time machine that [CAU-3](../../constraints/cau-3.md) says it is.
