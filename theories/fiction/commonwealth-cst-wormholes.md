# Commonwealth: CST wormholes with trains through them (Hamilton 2004)

*Two students open a wormhole to Mars and beat NASA there by minutes; three centuries later the same firm runs railways through them to hundreds of worlds.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L3 · **Notability:** N2 · **Coherence:** F2 · **Causality:** C-silent · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

Peter F. Hamilton's *Pandora's Star* (Pan Macmillan and Del Rey, 2004) opens in 2050 with Wilson Kime piloting the first crewed landing on Mars; when the crew steps out, a man is already standing there, breathing through an air hose that runs back through a wormhole to a laboratory in California **[HIGH] S3**. The wormhole's inventors are Nigel Sheldon and Ozzie Isaacs, who chose to test their machine by beating the astronauts to Mars, and the prologue is the moment the space programme becomes obsolete **[HIGH] S3**. By 2380 the two of them control Compression Space Transport, the company that owns the wormhole generators and runs the Commonwealth's transport as a railway: trains leave stations on one world, pass through a wormhole gateway and arrive on another, and the Commonwealth is several hundred planets across hundreds of light years joined that way **[HIGH] S3**. Wormholes are opened from the generator's end to a chosen destination, the far end need not be prepared, and the network's edge is set by how far a generator can reach **[MED] S3**.

The novel's plot begins where the wormholes stop. A pair of stars a thousand light years from Earth, about seven hundred and fifty beyond the Commonwealth's edge, are observed to be enclosed inside barriers in an instant, and the distance is beyond any wormhole's reach; so the Commonwealth builds its first starship, the *Second Chance*, which moves by a self-contained "flowing" wormhole, a wormhole that is not anchored at either end but carried with the ship so that the ship is always falling through it **[HIGH] S3**. Kime commands it. The novel also has a second, older network, the Silfen paths, walking routes through forests that lead between worlds without machinery, used by the alien Silfen and by Ozzie, and never explained **[HIGH] S3**. The sequel *Judas Unchained* (2005) continues the war that follows **[HIGH] S3**.

The drive as depicted, then, has two forms: a fixed gateway that opens from one end to a chosen point and carries trains, and a ship that carries its own wormhole ahead of it. Neither is given a metric, an energy source or a throat, beyond the fact that generators are large, expensive and planet-bound.

## Origin and lineage

Hamilton's wormholes are the traversable wormhole as everyday infrastructure, and the railway framing is his own: the book's transport system is Victorian, with stations, timetables and a monopoly operator **[MED] S3**. The word and the picture come from the post-1988 popular literature on [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), and the novel adds nothing to the physics and cites nothing; its interest is in what a society does with cheap gateways. The one-ended opening of a wormhole, from a generator to a distant point that has never been visited, is the move that [CON-2](../../constraints/con-2.md) prices and that the Commonwealth makes its foundation. The *Second Chance*'s flowing wormhole is the closest thing in this catalogue to a [Krasnikov tube](../physics/krasnikov-tube.md) used as an engine: a geometry carried along the route as the ship goes, though Krasnikov's tube is laid on the outbound leg to speed the return, and Hamilton's is a wormhole the ship perpetually falls into. Hamilton's later Void trilogy and *Salvation* sequence reuse the CST wormholes and are out of scope here.

## Which loophole, and why

L3, a shortcut: a gateway joins two points and a train crosses it. The starship is also L3, a wormhole moved with the ship rather than a bubble carrying it; the ship's own speed through the wormhole is never stated to exceed light and the shortcut is the wormhole, not the motion. The cousin is [Morris and Thorne 1988](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), with its bill of negative energy at the throat scaling with radius (and a train-sized throat is large), mass beyond engineering, a pre-laid route and a time machine once a mouth is moved. The Commonwealth pays none of it, opens its wormholes from one end, which the third forbids, and moves a mouth with every voyage of the *Second Chance*, which the fourth prices.

## The rules it keeps

- **Range.** Wormholes reach hundreds of light years, not a thousand; the Dyson Pair are out of reach and that is the plot's premise **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Gateways are fixed and owned.** Travel is by rail through CST stations, and CST's control of the gateways is the Commonwealth's politics **[HIGH] S3**.
- **Generators are planet-bound and costly.** Opening a wormhole is an industrial act, which is why the *Second Chance* is a novelty and why exploration beyond the edge needs a ship **[MED] S3**.
- **The ship falls through its own wormhole.** The flowing wormhole is applied consistently as the starship's method, with the *Second Chance* and its successors built on it **[HIGH] S3**.
- **The Silfen paths are not wormholes.** They are kept separate, walked rather than driven, and never explained **[HIGH] S3**.

F2: the rules are internally consistent, the setting is built carefully on them and none of them has physical content. Range, cost and ownership are the rules of a railway; what a wormhole is and what holds it open are never in the text. Not F1, because the rules bind the plot (a thousand light years is out of reach and stays out of reach until a ship is built); not F3, because the limitation is not a claim about the physics but about the budget.

## What it ignores

Every gateway in the Commonwealth connects two points hundreds of light years apart with no transit time, and messages go through them with the trains; the novel never asks in which frame that is a message into the past, never states a preferred frame and never builds or forbids a loop. The *Second Chance* moves a wormhole mouth across a thousand light years at relativistic speed and comes back, which is the Morris, Thorne and Yurtsever construction performed on every voyage, and nothing in the text notices **[HIGH] S3**. Absence dated: as of 2026-09-12 neither *Pandora's Star* nor *Judas Unchained* addresses the causality of the wormholes. C-silent. The energy, stability and tidal entries are untouched for the same reason as everywhere in this dossier: a gateway is a piece of infrastructure and its physics is never described.
