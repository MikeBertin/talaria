# Banks's Culture: the energy grid, infraspace and ultraspace (1987 onward)

*Ships that push against a grid of energy between nested universes, in a cosmology the author described as fake before anyone asked.*

**Kind:** fiction · **Loophole:** L4 · **Notability:** N2 · **Coherence:** F2 · **Causality:** C-silent · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The drive as depicted

Iain M. Banks's Culture novels run from *Consider Phlebas* (1987) to *The Hydrogen Sonata* (2012), and the drive is used everywhere and explained once, in an essay rather than a novel. "A Few Notes on the Culture" was posted to the newsgroup rec.arts.sf.written on 10 August 1994 on Banks's behalf by Ken MacLeod **[HIGH] S1** (the essay's own header). Its cosmology is this: our universe is a hypersphere, inside it are layers of younger and smaller hyperspheres and outside it older and larger ones, all expanding from a singularity that "exists all the time" and is exploding all the time; between each universe and the next is the Energy Grid, of which Banks writes "I have no idea what this is" but "it's what the Culture starships run on"; and because there is a grid on either side of our hypersphere there are two hyperspaces, infraspace within it and ultraspace without **[HIGH] S1** (essay, cosmology section). The essay's parenthesis, "(I said this was all fake)", sits in the middle of the paragraph **[HIGH] S1**.

The novels use the machinery consistently and add engineering. A ship enters infraspace or ultraspace by an induced singularity, and once there its engine fields reach down to the grid and take both power and traction from it; the engines use no reaction mass and need not be on the hull; a ship's acceleration and top speed depend on the ratio of engine mass to total mass, and a ship can grow or dismantle engine as needed **[MED] S3** (Wikipedia, "The Culture", technology section, citing *Use of Weapons* and *Excession*). *Excession* (1996) is the technical novel: the ship Sleeper Service rebuilds itself as mostly engine and reaches 233,000 times the speed of light, and the Excession of the title is an object with a direct connection to the grid, which is what makes it an event **[MED] S3** (Wikipedia; the novel from memory). Even at such speeds a crossing of Culture space takes years **[MED] S3**. Alongside the drive the Culture has Displacement, point-to-point transport of objects through small wormholes, with a range that falls with mass and a stated failure rate of one in sixty-one million (*Excession*) **[MED] S3**. Drones the size of a fist carry warp engines (*Consider Phlebas*) **[MED] S3**.

## Origin and lineage

Banks wrote the first Culture novels in the 1970s and published the first in 1987; the essay of 1994 was a reply to newsgroup questions and is the only place the cosmology is set out as such **[HIGH] S1**. The nested hyperspheres and the grid between them are Banks's own; the essay's disclaimer that it is fake is unusual in the genre and is the reason the drive is graded as it is here. Neither the essay nor any novel names a physics proposal, and no physics paper cites the Culture as an inspiration that this catalogue can source. The vocabulary of two hyperspaces on either side of our space, and of a bulk between universes, resembles the brane picture of the late 1990s, but the essay predates Randall and Sundrum by five years and the resemblance is not claimed as an influence in either direction **[HIGH] S1**.

## Which loophole, and why

L4, leave the manifold. The ship enters a different space (infraspace or ultraspace) and moves there by pushing on something that exists only between universes. The move is not a shortcut through our space and not a carried bubble, though the words "warp engine" and "engine fields" are used; the ship's speed is a speed through the other space. Displacement is a secondary L3 and is noted in the scoring where it bears.

The physics cousins are two. [Kaluza-Klein and the hyperspace reading](../physics/kaluza-klein-hyperspace.md) is the register's [MAN-1](../../constraints/man-1.md) worked through: extra dimensions in physics are compact or warped, share our light cone and have nothing between universes for an engine to push on. The [brane-bulk shortcut](../physics/brane-bulk-shortcuts.md) is the nearest picture physics has to Banks's grid, a bulk on either side of a brane universe through which a signal can cut ahead of light along the brane; its bill is a new warped dimension, early-universe energy densities to make the gain larger than one part in $10^{58}$ and the antitelephone unless the bulk fixes a frame; and it moves gravitons, not ships. Displacement's cousin is [Morris and Thorne](../physics/morris-thorne-1988.md), whose throat costs negative energy scaling with its radius.

## The rules it keeps

- **Two hyperspaces, one on each side**, named and used consistently from the essay onward **[HIGH] S1**.
- **Engines push on the grid and draw power from it**; no reaction mass; engine can be anywhere in the hull (*Excession*) **[MED] S3**.
- **Speed scales with the engine-to-mass ratio**, and the Sleeper Service's 233,000 c is earned by becoming mostly engine **[MED] S3**.
- **Distances still take years** at the top of the range **[MED] S3**.
- **Displacement has range limits and a failure rate**, and displacing living things is treated as a risk (*Excession*) **[MED] S3**.

The mark is F2. The rules are internally consistent across a dozen novels and have no physical content, and the author said so first. An F3 would need a stated limitation drawn from physics; Banks's limitations are drawn from the story's needs and labelled as such.

## What it ignores

Causality is never mentioned in the essay or, as far as this catalogue knows, in any Culture novel as of 2026-09-12. Ships at 233,000 c cross Culture space in years by one shared clock, Minds converse across it with lags measured in the story's own time and no instalment notices that an FTL trip in one frame is a trip into the past in another. No preferred frame is stated and no rule about hyperspace time is given, so the mark is C-silent rather than C-dodges. The register would also ask what the grid's energy is, what sign it has and what an induced singularity is; the essay's answer to all three is that Banks does not know, and that is scored as silence.
