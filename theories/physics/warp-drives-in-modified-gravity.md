# Warp drives in modified gravity

*Torsion, conformal gravity and other extensions move the warp bill from the matter side of the field equations to the geometry, and what each move costs.*

**Kind:** physics · **Loophole:** L2 · **Standing:** K3 · **Bill:** B-neg, B-new, B-mass, B-boot, B-caus · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-12

## The claim

Every theorem that prices a warp drive in negative energy is a theorem about the source of general relativity: the Einstein equations turn a statement about geometry (light rays converge) into a statement about matter (energy density is positive), and the warp metrics violate the geometric statement. The claim of this family of proposals is that general relativity is the wrong theory to price the drive with. Gravity has been tested where torsion, higher-curvature terms and extra scalar fields are negligible; in a theory that agrees with Einstein's everywhere it has been tested and differs where curvature or spin density is extreme, the same [Alcubierre](alcubierre-1994.md) metric can be sourced by matter that obeys the energy conditions, because the extra sector absorbs what in general relativity would have to be negative.

Two peer-reviewed papers make this claim with worked mathematics. DeBenedictis and Ilijic (2018) show that in Einstein-Cartan gravity, general relativity supplemented with torsion coupled to spin, the Alcubierre and [Van Den Broeck](van-den-broeck-1999.md) metrics can be sourced by a Weyssenhoff spin fluid whose stress-energy satisfies the weak and null energy conditions everywhere, with the ship still in free fall since torsion vanishes outside matter. Varieschi and Burstein (2013) show that in Mannheim's conformal (Weyl) gravity, a fourth-order theory, the Alcubierre metric with a suitable shaping function has non-negative energy density for every Eulerian observer at speeds up to about $2.5c$ and needs no exotic matter. In its strongest form the claim is that the exotic-matter objection to warp drives is not a fact about spacetime but an artefact of second-order, torsion-free field equations, and that the objection therefore stands or falls with general relativity's completeness at Planck-scale wall densities, which nobody has tested.

What the proponents do not claim: that the horizon, the bootstrap or the closed timelike curves go away (DeBenedictis and Ilijic say explicitly they do not address them); that the required spin densities or coupling constants are attainable; that conformal gravity or Einstein-Cartan theory is established. They claim that the bill can be moved. This dossier grades where it moves to.

## Origin and lineage

The idea is as old as the energy-condition objection. Alcubierre's 1994 letter says exotic matter is needed "just as it happens with wormholes"; the [traversable wormhole](morris-thorne-1988.md) literature then spent two decades showing that in extended theories of gravity the matter threading a throat can be ordinary while "the effective stress-energy tensor containing higher order curvature derivatives" carries the null energy condition violation, in f(R) gravity (Lobo and Oliveira 2009), in Brans-Dicke theory and in curvature-matter couplings **[HIGH] S1**. The general point that energy conditions in extended theories apply to an effective fluid mixing matter and geometry, and that it is "somewhat misleading" to read them as conditions on matter, is made by Capozziello, Lobo and Mimoso (2015) for scalar-tensor, f(R) and Gauss-Bonnet gravity **[HIGH] S1**.

For warp drives specifically the record is thin. Varieschi and Burstein, Loyola Marymount, "Conformal gravity and the Alcubierre warp drive metric", ISRN Astronomy and Astrophysics 2013, 482734, arXiv:1208.3706 **[HIGH] S1**. DeBenedictis (Simon Fraser) and Ilijic (Zagreb), "Energy condition respecting warp drives: the role of spin in Einstein-Cartan theory", Class. Quantum Grav. 35, 215001 (2018), arXiv:1807.09745 **[HIGH] S1**. A 2026 preprint on ScienceOpen recomputes the Einstein-Cartan thresholds in closed form and compares them with nuclear matter **[LOW] S1**. Santiago, Schuster and Visser (2022) supply the general objection ([ENE-5](../../constraints/ene-5.md)). Searches of arXiv on 2026-09-12 for "warp drive" together with f(R), Brans-Dicke, scalar-tensor, Gauss-Bonnet, teleparallel or f(T) returned no paper applying a warp metric in any of those theories; the only "warp drive" and "modified gravity" hit is Santiago, Schuster and Visser's abstract. That absence is a finding: the f(R) and Brans-Dicke warp drives that get invoked in conversation do not exist in the literature, only their wormhole cousins do. Three adjacent items are recorded for completeness: Rodal (2025) on why a spatially varying gravitational coupling becomes a scalar-tensor theory constrained by the Solar System **[LOW] S1**; Jusufi and Lobo (2026) on a T-duality-regularised Alcubierre profile that keeps its exotic matter **[LOW] S1**; and Joseph (2022) on a Bohmian-gravity reformulation, on physics.gen-ph without a journal **[LOW] S1**. The register lists modified gravity as an escape hatch of [ENE-2](../../constraints/ene-2.md); this is the dossier that scores the hatch.

## The mechanism

Units $G = c = 1$ unless stated. **The general move.** Most extended theories can be rearranged as

$$G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\,T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\bigl(T^{\mathrm{matter}}_{\mu\nu} + T^{\mathrm{geom}}_{\mu\nu}\bigr),$$

where $T^{\mathrm{geom}}$ collects torsion-squared terms, higher derivatives of curvature or scalar-field stresses. The Alcubierre geometry fixes the left side. General relativity says the right side is $T^{\mathrm{matter}}$ and it is negative in the wall. An extended theory lets $T^{\mathrm{matter}}$ be positive provided $T^{\mathrm{geom}}$ is negative enough there. Santiago, Schuster and Visser state the limit of this: energy conditions are, through the field equations, "typically immediately converted into purely geometrical convergence conditions", the null convergence condition $R_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b \geq 0$ and the timelike convergence condition $R_{ab}V^aV^b \geq 0$, and "even in modified gravity, physically reasonable warp drives will still violate the purely geometrical null convergence condition and the timelike convergence condition which, in turn, will place very strong constraints on any modified-gravity warp drive" **[HIGH] S1**. The Raychaudhuri equation does not know which theory sourced the metric. Whatever theory one adopts, the Alcubierre wall makes null rays diverge where general relativity would need negative energy to make them do so, and the theorems of [ENE-2](../../constraints/ene-2.md) (Olum's, Gao and Wald's) are proved from that focusing, with the energy condition entering only as the general-relativistic name for it. Modified gravity therefore buys a positive matter sector at the cost of a geometric sector that carries the negative part, and the question for each theory is what that sector is made of and what it costs.

**Einstein-Cartan.** The connection carries torsion $T^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$; varying the action with respect to the metric and the contorsion gives two equations, one relating the full Einstein tensor and the modified torsion (superpotential) $S^{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ to the stress-energy, the other setting $S^{\alpha\beta\gamma} = \kappa\,\tau^{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ with $\tau$ the spin tensor. Torsion therefore vanishes wherever spin vanishes: outside matter the theory is general relativity and test particles follow its geodesics **[HIGH] S1**. For a Weyssenhoff fluid, $\tau_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\gamma} = \tau_{\alpha\beta}u^\gamma$ with the Frenkel condition $\tau_{\alpha\beta}u^\beta = 0$, and after macroscopic averaging of unpolarised spins the quadratic terms survive, giving

$$G_{\mu\nu}\bigl[\{\}\bigr] - \kappa^2 s^2\bigl(-2u_\mu u_\nu - g_{\mu\nu}\bigr) = \kappa\,T_{\mu\nu}, \qquad s^2 := \tau_{\alpha\beta}\tau^{\alpha\beta},$$

with $G_{\mu\nu}[\{\}]$ the Einstein tensor of the Christoffel connection, that is, the ordinary Einstein tensor of the Alcubierre metric **[HIGH] S1**. The weak and null conditions on the matter then read

$$T_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu = \frac{1}{\kappa}\Bigl[G_{\mu\nu}\bigl[\{\}\bigr] - \kappa^2 s^2\bigl(-2u_\mu u_\nu - g_{\mu\nu}\bigr)\Bigr]v^\mu v^\nu \;\geq\; 0 .$$

The first term is the general-relativistic energy density, negative in the wall. The second is $\kappa s^2\,\chi_{\mu\nu}v^\mu v^\nu$ with $\chi_{\mu\nu} = 2u_\mu u_\nu + g_{\mu\nu}$, and for normalised isochronous timelike $u$, $v$ this equals $2(u_\mu v^\mu)^2 - 1 \geq 1 > 0$ by the reversed Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, with the null case following. So "the WEC may be made positive for sufficiently large $s^2$": that is the whole theorem, and it is exact **[HIGH] S1**. Two caveats the authors record: a perfect or anisotropic fluid is algebraically incompatible with the warp metric's Segre type, so a spinless auxiliary structure must be added to the matter, and $T_{\mu\nu}$ is otherwise "whatever is required in order to create the warp drive". The fluid four-velocity mimics the ship's but is not in free fall.

The numbers, minimising $s^2$ over the worst observer at each point for $v_s = 1.2$: the peak requirement is $\kappa^2 s^2 \approx 5\ \mathrm{m}^{-2}$, an angular momentum density $L \approx 3.6 \times 10^{34}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, or about $3.5 \times 10^{68}\,\hbar$ per cubic metre, which for spin-$\hbar$ carriers is $3.5 \times 10^{68}$ particles per cubic metre; the accompanying Eulerian energy density peaks near $0.3\ \mathrm{m}^{-2}$, about $3.64 \times 10^{43}$ J per cubic metre or a mass density of $4 \times 10^{26}$ kg per cubic metre **[HIGH] S1**. The authors' aside: "As is often the case with exotic solutions in gravitational field theory, the devil is in the details." A monochromatic photon field carrying that spin would need a field of order $9 \times 10^{22}\sqrt{\omega}$ V/m and would lose U(1) gauge invariance by coupling to torsion; high-spin composites are unstable and too massive; fundamental high-spin fields are problematic to couple **[HIGH] S1**. For the Van Den Broeck pocket with $P = 3$ fm, $\tilde P = 1$ fm, $\alpha = 5$, the peak is $\kappa^2 s^2 \approx 150\ \mathrm{fm}^{-2}$, $L \approx 1.9 \times 10^{35}$ kg per femtometre per second, about $10^{84}$ spin-one particles per cubic metre, with a mass density of about $8 \times 10^{11}$ kg per cubic femtometre, in a much smaller volume **[HIGH] S1**. The 2026 preprint's closed-form threshold for $R = 10$ m, $\sigma = 100\ \mathrm{m}^{-1}$, $v_s = 0.5c$ is about $4.0 \times 10^{35}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$, which it puts at 25.7 orders of magnitude beyond fully polarised nuclear-saturation matter **[LOW] S1**.

**Conformal gravity.** The action is the square of the Weyl tensor, $I_W = -\alpha_g\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,C_{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}C^{\lambda\mu\nu\kappa}$, invariant under local rescalings $g \to \Omega^2 g$, and the field equations are Bach's, $W_{\mu\nu} = T_{\mu\nu}/4\alpha_g$, with the Bach tensor

$$W_{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}R^{;\lambda}{}_{;\lambda} + \tfrac{2}{3}R_{;\mu;\nu} + R_{\mu\nu}{}^{;\lambda}{}_{;\lambda} - R_\mu{}^\lambda{}_{;\nu;\lambda} - R_\nu{}^\lambda{}_{;\mu;\lambda} + \tfrac{2}{3}R\,R_{\mu\nu} - 2R_\mu{}^\lambda R_{\lambda\nu} + \tfrac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R_{\lambda\rho}R^{\lambda\rho} - \tfrac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}R^2,$$

fourth order in the metric **[HIGH] S1**. The stress-energy is read off as $T_{\mu\nu} = 4\alpha_g W_{\mu\nu}$ for the Alcubierre metric with two shaping functions: Alcubierre's tanh profile and a polynomial "Hartle shaping function" with parameter $m = 4$. In general relativity both give Alcubierre's negative $T^{00}$. In conformal gravity with $\alpha_g = 1$ the tanh profile gives a $T^{00}$ that is "in part positive and in part negative, thus still violating the WEC", while the Hartle profile gives a $T^{00}$ that is "completely non-negative" for $v_s = 1$, $R = 1$; scanning $v_s$ from $0.25c$ to $3c$, the density stays non-negative up to $v_s \approx 2.5c$ and develops negative lobes beyond **[HIGH] S1**. The strong condition is satisfied, the dominant condition is violated in the case examined. Integrating over a bubble of $R = 100$ m at $v_s = c$ gives $E = 1.86 \times 10^{10}\,\alpha_g$ erg; with the only published value of the coupling, Mannheim's $\alpha_g = 3.29 \times 10^{82}$ erg s, this is $E = 6.12 \times 10^{92}$ erg $= 3.42 \times 10^{38}$ solar masses of ordinary matter, which the authors note might be reduced by a factor of $10^{68}$ to $10^{80}$ if the microscopic coupling differs from the macroscopic one **[HIGH] S1**. The theory itself is contested: Flanagan argues its conformal invariance is an artefact of the variables and that in the weak-field, slow-motion limit it disagrees with general relativity and is ruled out by Solar System observations **[MED] S1**, a conclusion Mannheim disputes in the literature the paper cites.

**What the mechanism shows in one line.** In both theories the Alcubierre metric is unchanged, so every constraint that is about the metric (horizon, bootstrap, closed timelike curves, hazards, the null convergence condition) is unchanged, and the negative quantity that general relativity calls energy density is renamed: spin-squared torsion stress in one theory, Bach-tensor curvature stress in the other.

## What it costs

**B-neg.** The geometric null convergence condition is violated regardless of theory, so the effective source is negative in the wall in every theory; the matter is positive only because a geometric term has been given the sign general relativity assigned to the matter. In the specific models the conditions are not even fully rescued: conformal gravity violates the dominant condition in the case computed, and the Einstein-Cartan spin fluid needs an auxiliary spinless structure whose stresses are "whatever is required" ([ENE-1](../../constraints/ene-1.md), [ENE-5](../../constraints/ene-5.md)).

**B-new.** Torsion coupled to spin, or a fourth-order conformally invariant gravitational action, neither of which is in general relativity or has been observed; in the Einstein-Cartan case the carrier of $10^{68}\,\hbar$ per cubic metre has no candidate the authors find satisfactory, and a photon carrier would break U(1) gauge invariance.

**B-mass.** Spin densities of $10^{68}\,\hbar$ per cubic metre at mass densities of $4 \times 10^{26}$ kg per cubic metre for a $v_s = 1.2$ Alcubierre bubble, $10^{84}$ spin-one particles per cubic metre for the pocket; or $3.4 \times 10^{38}$ solar masses of ordinary matter in conformal gravity at the published coupling. These are positive but not payable.

**B-boot.** The horizon is a property of the metric and DeBenedictis and Ilijic acknowledge it remains; the wall matter's leading edge is spacelike for $v_s > 1$ whatever sources it ([CON-1](../../constraints/con-1.md), [CON-2](../../constraints/con-2.md)).

**B-caus.** Everett's two-bubble loop uses only the metric ([CAU-3](../../constraints/cau-3.md)); nothing in either theory picks a frame.

## Status of the argument

- **2009.** Lobo and Oliveira construct f(R) wormholes threaded by matter obeying the energy conditions, with the higher-order curvature terms as the "gravitational fluid" that violates the null condition; the wormhole proof of concept for the whole strategy **[HIGH] S1**. Brans-Dicke wormholes are discussed in the same paper's revision.
- **2013.** Varieschi and Burstein: conformal gravity, Hartle shaping function, non-negative $T^{00}$ up to $2.5c$, dominant condition violated, $3.4 \times 10^{38}$ solar masses at Mannheim's coupling **[HIGH] S1**. No peer-reviewed response as of 2026-09-12. The journal, ISRN Astronomy and Astrophysics, was a Hindawi title since discontinued. Flanagan's 2006 argument that the theory fails Solar System tests predates and is not addressed by the paper **[MED] S1**.
- **2015.** Capozziello, Lobo and Mimoso: energy conditions in extended theories apply to an effective fluid mixing matter and geometry, and "repulsive gravity may occur for standard matter" **[HIGH] S1**.
- **2018.** DeBenedictis and Ilijic: Einstein-Cartan spin fluid, exact theorem that the weak and null conditions can be satisfied for large enough $s^2$, minimum spin profiles for the Alcubierre and Van Den Broeck metrics, $10^{68}\,\hbar$ per cubic metre and $4 \times 10^{26}$ kg per cubic metre, "difficult to imagine how such spin densities may be achieved" **[HIGH] S1**. No peer-reviewed response as of 2026-09-12.
- **2022.** Santiago, Schuster and Visser: generic warp drives violate the null energy condition, and in modified gravity still violate the null and timelike convergence conditions, which "will place very strong constraints on any modified-gravity warp drive" **[HIGH] S1**. This is the standing general objection and no modified-gravity paper has answered it.
- **2022.** Joseph, "Alcubierre warp drive in Bohmian quantum gravity", maps the exotic matter into a fourth-order conformal wave equation for a quantum density; physics.gen-ph preprint, no journal **[LOW] S1**.
- **2024.** Garattini and Zatrimaylov show, within general relativity, that a Schwarzschild background reduces the weak and null violations of a warp bubble; not modified gravity, but the same instinct of borrowing the violation from elsewhere **[HIGH] S1**.
- **2025.** Rodal argues that a spatially varying gravitational coupling, proposed for "metamaterial" low-energy warp drives, clashes with the contracted Bianchi identity, and that making it dynamical yields a scalar-tensor theory whose coupling is bounded by Solar System and pulsar timing to levels that make the application infeasible **[LOW] S1**. This is the closest thing on arXiv to a Brans-Dicke warp drive, and it is a no-go.
- **2026.** Jusufi and Lobo build a T-duality-inspired Alcubierre profile with a zero-point length that removes the thin-wall divergence; they stress it is an effective construction, not a modified field equation, and that exotic matter persists **[LOW] S1**. A ScienceOpen preprint recomputes Einstein-Cartan thresholds and finds them 25.7 orders of magnitude beyond polarised nuclear matter **[LOW] S1**. Non-peer-reviewed "torsion-enhanced" warp drives, grafted onto the subluminal shell of [Fuchs et al. (2024)](fuchs-constant-velocity-2024.md), circulate on Academia.edu and PhilArchive; none has a journal and none is scored here **[LOW] S5**.
- **2026-09-12.** No f(R), Brans-Dicke, Gauss-Bonnet or teleparallel warp drive exists in the literature. The two that do exist, Einstein-Cartan and conformal gravity, keep the metric and rename the negative term. The escape hatch ENE-2 lists is real, is used by two peer-reviewed papers, and leads to a room with the same horizon, the same time machine and a bill in a different currency.
